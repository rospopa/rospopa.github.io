How to use submarket labels

Industrial submarket names are shorthand. They help brokers, lenders, owners, and tenants talk about geography, but they are not zoning districts, tax districts, or municipal boundaries. A data provider may draw “O’Hare,” “I-55,” or “Joliet” differently from a broker or municipality.

Before underwriting a 10,000 to 100,000 square foot building, verify the parcel’s county and municipality using the nine-county industrial research hub. Then match the address to zoning, permitted use, truck routes, taxes, and utilities. A building can be marketed under one corridor name while legally sitting in a different taxing body or approval jurisdiction.

Practical use

Use labels for search and tours; use legal records for closing, financing, zoning, environmental review, and municipal approvals.

Corridors and clusters described by geography

O?Hare corridor

Elk Grove Village, Bensenville, Wood Dale, Franklin Park, Schiller Park, and Des Plaines are tied to I-90, I-294, I-190, and O?Hare air cargo. Verify county, municipality, truck circulation, and airport-adjacent restrictions.

North Cook and Lake County

Northbrook, Wheeling, Buffalo Grove, Lincolnshire, Libertyville, Gurnee, and Waukegan relate to I-94, US-41, and local arterials. Lake County means Illinois, not Indiana.

Northwest and I-90

Schaumburg, Hoffman Estates, Elgin, Huntley, and the Belvidere direction follow I-90. Boone County is outside scope, and Elgin crosses county lines.

Central DuPage, I-355 and I-88

Addison, Carol Stream, West Chicago, Glendale Heights, Lombard, Naperville, and Aurora relate to I-355, I-88, North Avenue, Roosevelt Road, and DuPage Airport. Aurora and Naperville cross county lines.

West Cook, I-290 and inner I-55

Bedford Park, McCook, Hodgkins, Cicero, Bellwood, Melrose Park, and Northlake form an infill logistics belt near BNSF Cicero, CSX Bedford Park, and Union Pacific Global 2.

I-55 corridor

Bolingbrook, Romeoville, Woodridge, Lemont, and Plainfield are commonly grouped along I-55, I-355, and nearby arterials across Will, DuPage, and Cook County.

I-80 and Joliet area

Joliet, Elwood, Channahon, Minooka, Shorewood, University Park, and Monee are oriented to I-80 and I-55. BNSF Logistics Park Chicago and Union Pacific Global 4 are key reference points.

South Cook, I-57 and I-294

Alsip, Bridgeview, Chicago Heights, Harvey, Markham, Matteson, and South Holland sit near I-57, I-294, I-80, and Calumet-area rail infrastructure, including Norfolk Southern, CN, and other rail references.

City of Chicago industrial corridors

The City of Chicago uses official industrial corridor planning and Planned Manufacturing District tools. See the Industrial Corridor Modernization Initiative.

Kane, Kendall, DeKalb, McHenry, and Grundy outer markets

Aurora, Batavia, Geneva, St. Charles, Yorkville, Oswego, Plano, DeKalb, Sycamore, Woodstock, Crystal Lake, and Morris are smaller clusters tied to I-88, the I-39 direction, and I-80 west.

Freight infrastructure overview

Freight infrastructure should be treated as a location attribute, not a guarantee. A building can be near a terminal or airport and still have poor truck access, wrong zoning, inadequate loading, or limited yard depth.

How location factors enter underwriting

For 10,000 to 100,000 square foot industrial buildings, location underwriting is usually specific and practical. A small user or private investor cares about the actual drive, dock, labor, and approval path more than a broad corridor label.

Factors to test

  • Labor access: shift timing, transit availability, parking, and commute patterns.
  • Truck routes: municipal restrictions, bridge clearances, road weight limits, turning movements, and neighborhood constraints.
  • Last-mile versus regional distribution: customer proximity, expressway access, driver hours, and delivery window reliability.
  • Tollway costs: repeated toll exposure can affect operating cost even when the route is efficient.
  • Building function: clear height, loading, truck court, yard, power, sprinklers, office ratio, and expansion area. See the industrial building guide.

Nearby infrastructure does not replace property-level diligence. A building can sit near I-55 and still have a difficult left turn, shallow dock apron, or ordinance that limits trailer storage.

Verification steps before relying on a submarket

Use this sequence before marketing a building, signing an LOI, or budgeting improvements.

Checklist

  1. Confirm the address, PIN, county, municipality, township, school district, and fire district in the county hub.
  2. Verify zoning, legal nonconforming status, outdoor storage, parking, truck routes, and hours of operation with the municipality.
  3. Review utilities: electric service, gas, water, sanitary, stormwater, fire protection, and available capacity.
  4. Check access: curb cuts, easements, shared drives, rail rights, signal timing, bridge limits, and municipal truck-route maps.
  5. Compare the building to the user need: loading, clear height, power, office finish, yard, floor condition, and expansion land.
  6. Review taxes, incentives, TIFs, enterprise zones, and transfer taxes with the Illinois industrial property tax guide.
