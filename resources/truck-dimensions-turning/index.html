Truck dimensions and turning paths

Use these sourced examples to ask better questions about loading, gates, driveways, yards, and truck courts. Cargo capacity, road-legal dimensions, and space needed to maneuver are different measurements.

Published and sources checked October 6, 2026. Metric conversions are rounded.

In brief

  • A 53-foot trailer is not a 53-foot tractor-trailer combination. A box-truck body size is not the overall truck length.
  • A container's dimensions alone do not describe the tractor, chassis, loaded height, or turning envelope.
  • There is no universal truck turning radius or safe truck-court depth. Require fleet-specific swept-path review by a qualified engineer.

Tractors and semitrailers

A tractor alone includes the cab and power unit. Its wheelbase, axle layout, sleeper, and fifth-wheel position vary. A tractor plus semitrailer is an articulated combination; overall length depends on both vehicles and their connection geometry. Do not add a guessed tractor length to the trailer label or use that label as the rig's overall length.

Semitrailer length references, not overall combination dimensions
ReferenceImperialMetricWhat the number means
48-foot semitrailer48 ft14.63 mFHWA describes the minimum semitrailer length states must allow on the National Network and reasonable access routes, subject to grandfathered provisions. Not interior cargo length.
53-foot semitrailer53 ft16.15 mFHWA's Illinois grandfathered-length table lists 53 ft. This is the trailer length reference, not an overall rig length or a turning template.

FHWA states that there is no federal overall-length limit for tractor-semitrailer combinations on the National Network and reasonable access routes. Its general commercial-vehicle width limit is 102 in (8 ft 6 in; approximately 2.6 m), with regulatory exceptions. Neither statement proves a particular vehicle can use a particular local road or driveway. Confirm current Illinois rules, route restrictions, permits, axle positions, and actual fleet dimensions.

There is no federal commercial-vehicle height requirement. FHWA describes most state limits as 13 ft 6 in to 14 ft (approximately 4.11 to 4.27 m), with lower road clearances and exceptions. This is not a current Illinois height authorization; check the actual route and current state/local rules.

A straight truck carries its cargo body on the same vehicle chassis as its cab. The two verified rental examples below are interior cargo-box dimensions, not exterior bodies, door openings, overall vehicle lengths, or roof heights.

Verify actual overall length, exterior width including protrusions, loaded and unloaded height, wheelbase, rear overhang, door opening, and liftgate deployment separately.

Containers and chassis

20-foot and 40-foot dry containers, including high-cube equipment, are containers alone, not complete road vehicles. Nominal length names are not usable interior dimensions. A high cube has a taller container body; the chassis deck, tires, suspension, and loading condition contribute additional height when carried on the road.

Metric values above convert those published nominal sizes; they are not precise ISO manufacturing dimensions or measurements of a particular unit. None includes a chassis or tractor. Obtain the actual equipment drawing for clearance work.

Specify the carrier's exact equipment type and request its exterior, interior, and door-opening drawing. For a chassis combination, also obtain chassis length, axle positions, container placement, loaded height, and tractor geometry. Do not derive overall vehicle height or turning clearance from the container label. ISO 668 is the dimensional/classification standard; its full specification is not reproduced here.

Turning circle, swept path, and offtracking

Turning circle diameter vs. radius
Diameter spans a complete circle; radius is half that diameter only when both refer to the same circle and measurement convention. A curb-to-curb wheel path is not a wall-to-wall body envelope. Ask which reference point, steering lock, speed, and maneuver the supplier measured.
Swept path and outer sweep
A swept path is the area occupied by the vehicle through a specific maneuver. Its outer boundary must account for front corners, mirrors, body overhang, and trailer swing, not just a tire track.
Offtracking
Rear wheels and a trailer do not follow the tractor's front-wheel path in a low-speed turn; they commonly track inward. Trailer length, wheelbase, axle locations, articulation, steering, and approach angle change the path.
Design vehicle
AASHTO design-vehicle labels such as WB-62 and WB-67 describe particular geometric templates, not interchangeable fleet measurements or a universal radius. A qualified engineer must select a suitable current design vehicle and check it against the site's actual fleet and maneuvers.

No numerical turning radius is supplied here because the cited fleet specifications do not establish one for a complete configuration. Forward circulation, reversing into a dock, simultaneous opposing traffic, and queuing require different checks. FHWA road-size limits are not turning-path templates.

Site-review checklist

  1. Identify the fleet: exact tractor, trailer, straight-truck, or container/chassis configuration, wheelbases, axle positions, body projections, height, and operating loads.
  2. Survey the constraints: gate openings, curb returns, driveways, dock angles, court depth, grade changes, fences, bollards, poles, parked vehicles, easements, fire access, snow storage, and overhead obstructions.
  3. Test the maneuver: have a qualified civil/transportation engineer perform fleet-specific swept-path analysis for entry, turns, backing, unloading, and exit, including required operational margins and conflicts.
  4. Check access and operation: local truck routes, bridge/underpass restrictions, delivery rules, pavement capacity, loading equipment, and municipal approvals. Legal road width or nominal cargo size is not a guaranteed safe clearance.

Primary sources and limitations

  • FHWA: Federal Size Regulations for Commercial Motor Vehicles, including the Illinois semitrailer grandfathered-length table. This explanatory report is not a current local-route approval; actual regulations govern.
  • Penske 16-foot truck and Penske 26-foot truck: approximate interior dimensions that vary by manufacturer.
  • Penske commercial light-duty trucks: published fleet labels, not overall vehicle drawings.
  • ISO 668 catalog: container classification/dimension standard; full tables require access to the standard.
  • Maersk dry equipment specifications (PDF): published nominal outside-size classes for standard and high-cube steel containers, with separate interior and door-opening specifications.
  • AASHTO publications: the Green Book contains design-vehicle guidance. Its paid dimensional/turning templates are not reproduced or replaced with guessed values.
Scope. Educational reference, not engineering, construction, traffic, safety, or legal advice. No table here authorizes a clearance, maneuver, vehicle movement, or site design.
