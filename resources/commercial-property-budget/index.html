How to budget expenses, reserves and capital for a commercial building

A profitable operating statement may not fund a roof, vacant suite or loan maturity. Connect monthly operations, tenant recoveries, capital timing and financing without mixing them.

Educational, not an appraisal or investment, tax, legal, engineering or insurance advice. Verify your property, leases and current rules with qualified advisers.

In brief

  • Maintain an operating budget and a separate capital/liquidity plan.
  • Normalize expense benchmarks for area, occupancy, use and lease basis.
  • Reserves are cash set aside; spending and tax deductions are different events.
  • Tax treatment is a separate CPA analysis, not an NOI shortcut.
  • Owner lens: Keep unrestricted operating liquidity distinct from lender-controlled escrows and earmarked reserves. Stress near-term debt maturity and a major repair occurring in the same period as vacancy.

What belongs in a commercial operating budget?

Use invoices, signed leases and monthly trailing statements. Budget tenant base rent, recoveries, collection loss and ancillary income. Track taxes, insurance, utilities, repairs, snow, waste, grounds, management and recurring services; schedule renewals and seasonal bills by due date and compare annually.

For Chicagoland industrial assets, review winter service and heat, roof/drains, loading equipment and paving alongside tenancy. Separate tenant-paid utilities from landlord service. Include tax notices, appeal status and insurance-renewal assumptions instead of extrapolating a quiet year.

Monthly, reconcile budget and actual price, usage and timing, update collections and forecast. Tax refunds and insurance recoveries are not recurring income. NOI reconciliation preserves the operating basis; cash forecasts show liquidity timing.

How do I benchmark operating expenses without misleading comparisons?

Define whether cost per square foot uses rentable, occupied or gross area. Compare property type, heated area, tenant use, service level, period and gross/net lease basis. Separate capital and one-time costs. Utility use per operating hour or occupied area may reveal more than dollars alone.

Use normalized history, comparable vendor scopes and verified peer data. This guide provides no Chicago expense ratios or market rates. A low ratio may reflect deferred maintenance, tenant-paid costs or missing management fees, not efficiency. Label sources and adjustments.

Federal IRS tangible-property rules distinguish ordinary expenses from capitalized amounts, including betterments, restorations and adaptations. Dollar size alone does not decide classification. Safe harbors and elections have conditions; do not relabel improvements as repairs to reduce NOI or taxable income.

How much reserve cash does a commercial owner need?

No universal reserve percentage suffices. Schedule condition, timing, installed replacement estimates, lease responsibility and contingencies, plus vacancy carry, TI/LC, deductibles, tax timing and loan maturity. Separate lender-restricted reserves from readily available cash.

Illustrative arithmetic, not a reserve recommendation: a supported $120,000 project estimate, $20,000 already dedicated and five years to completion imply $20,000 annual straight-line funding before inflation, interest or contingencies. An earlier failure requires cash sooner. Do not count both annual contributions and the eventual project as expenses in the same cash-flow model.

Depreciation, cost segregation and 1031: what should I ask my CPA?

Depreciation allocates eligible capital basis over its recovery period; it is not current-year cash spending and is excluded from ordinary property NOI. Land is not depreciable. Basis allocation, placed-in-service dates, classification, elections and current law matter. See IRS Publication 946, not a promised deduction.

A cost-segregation study identifies supported components and tax classifications/recovery periods; it is neither an automatic percentage write-off nor an operating expense. Ask about study quality, basis reconciliation, timing, costs and recapture on sale. The IRS audit-guide directory links the Cost Segregation guide and warns that law may change after publication.

Section 1031 may defer, not erase, qualifying gain on business/investment real-property exchanges. Property held primarily for sale does not qualify; cash or other non-like-kind property may create taxable gain. Before closing, discuss ownership, qualified-intermediary arrangements, identification and completion deadlines, debt and state taxes. See sale/1031 preparation and the IRS real-estate exchange overview. Consult a CPA and attorney; no tax result is promised.

Refinancing and debt costs belong in the liquidity plan

Track interest, amortization, fees, escrows, covenants, maturity, recourse and prepayment. DSCR compares a lender-defined income measure with debt service; LTV compares debt with its value basis; debt yield compares defined NOI with loan balance. No universal qualifying threshold applies.

Refinancing plans do not erase balloon balances. Test lower proceeds, higher borrowing costs and delayed closing; calculate cash gaps after payoff and fees. Use the debt-service calculator and financing guide and levered versus unlevered projections, not a duplicate calculator.

Owner questions and answers

What is included in commercial operating expenses?

Typically taxes, property insurance, owner-paid utilities, recurring repairs/services and management. Debt service, depreciation and major capital outlays are separate from ordinary NOI. State reserve and lease-recovery conventions.

Are reserves the same as capital spending?

No. Reserves earmark cash; capital spending pays for work. Reconcile lender underwriting conventions to avoid double-counting.

Does cost segregation increase NOI?

No. It concerns tax depreciation timing and classification, not rental operating income. Review eligibility, study quality, current law and later recapture with a CPA.
