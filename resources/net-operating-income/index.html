How is net operating income calculated for commercial property?

Educational, not an appraisal or investment, tax, legal, engineering or insurance advice. Verify your property, leases and current rules with qualified advisers.

By Pavlo Rospopa · Last updated October 10, 2026

In brief

NOI measures property operations, not cash in the bank. Reconcile leases, collections, expenses and recoveries on a consistent annual basis.

  • Effective gross income minus property operating expenses equals NOI.
  • Debt service, depreciation, owner income taxes and capital spending are not ordinary NOI expenses.
  • A lease recovery is income to the owner, not permission to hide the matching expense.
  • Compare trailing, current and stabilized NOI separately.
  • Owner lens: When comparing an owner-occupied building with an investment, show a supportable market rent for the occupied space and keep business costs separate from property expenses; otherwise the comparison can overstate or understate NOI.

What is NOI, and what is included?

Net operating income = effective gross income (EGI) minus operating expenses. Start with full-occupancy rent on the chosen lease or market basis, less vacancy, free rent and collection losses, plus other property income and recoveries. Reconcile rent roll, signed amendments, receivables and ledger.

Typical expenses are real estate taxes, building insurance, owner-paid utilities, repairs, maintenance, contracted services and management. When comparing professionally managed investments, include a supported management allowance even if the owner self-manages. Owner business revenue, tenant sales, refundable deposits and loan proceeds are not property rental income.

NOI usually excludes mortgage principal and interest, depreciation, owner income taxes, acquisition costs, tenant improvements, leasing commissions and major capital replacements. Replacement reserves may sit below NOI or enter a lender-adjusted measure. State and reconcile the convention; do not compare incompatible figures.

How do NNN, modified-gross and gross leases change NOI?

For triple-net leases, record recoverable taxes, insurance and maintenance as expenses and collectible reimbursements as income, deducting the unrecovered share. “NNN” does not define eligible repairs, capital items, administration or reassessment increases.

Gross leases leave more expense risk with the owner; modified gross may use a base year, expense stop or negotiated split. Check each tenant’s exclusions, caps, gross-up clauses, audit rights and reconciliation deadlines. A vacant suite may not reimburse its share. See lease expense allocation and CAM reconciliation and cost controls.

How do buyers and lenders re-underwrite NOI?

Separate trailing-12-month results, a forward annual estimate and stabilized NOI. Buyers may remove one-time income, replace related-party service costs with supported costs, normalize maintenance, adjust management fees, budget insurance renewal and test future Illinois taxes. No adjustment is automatic; retain its evidence and effective date.

After lease expiry, model downtime, tenant improvements and commissions. Stabilized NOI at full occupancy is not current collected income. The DCF guide models costs through a hold; the Illinois tax guide covers reassessment and appeals.

Common mistakes that inflate NOI

  • Counting recoveries without the matching expense.
  • Ignoring free rent, arrears, nonrecoverable CAM or approaching vacancy.
  • Calling a major roof replacement routine maintenance.
  • Treating a low historical tax bill as guaranteed.
  • Mixing cash and accrual reporting or area denominators without reconciliation.

What is a good NOI, and how does it affect value?

No universal “good” NOI or margin exists. Compare type, condition, leases, occupancy and reporting convention, not totals alone for a 10,000-SF gross-leased building versus a 100,000-SF net-leased warehouse.

Direct capitalization uses value = NOI / supported cap rate. Durable NOI may increase indicated value, but vacancy risk, lease term, functionality and capital needs affect the rate and buyer adjustments. Owner-paid energy savings fully reimbursed by a tenant may lower tenant costs without adding owner NOI. Improve budget discipline and reserves, not promise a fixed uplift.

Owner questions and answers

Does NOI include mortgage payments?

No. Principal and interest are financing cash flows below ordinary NOI; lenders may adjust income for debt sizing.

Is a roof replacement deducted from NOI?

A major replacement is generally a capital cash need presented separately from NOI; routine repair treatment depends on the work and accounting convention. Tax classification is a separate CPA review.

What is a good NOI for a commercial property?

There is no universal figure. Compare documented income and expenses on the same period, occupancy, lease and reserve basis, then consider condition and future leasing costs.
