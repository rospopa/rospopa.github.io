Property taxes on Illinois industrial real estate

How Illinois property tax works at a high level

Illinois industrial property taxes are local, within a state-supervised system: assessment, equalization, levy, rate, bill, and collection. Check each step against the parcel’s county, municipality, school district, and special districts.

From assessment to bill

Assessment
The assessor estimates taxable value. Outside Cook County, Illinois generally assesses at 33⅓% of fair cash value before equalization and exemptions; Cook County uses its own classification levels.
Equalization
An equalization factor, or multiplier, may adjust assessments for greater uniformity. See assessed value and equalization factor.
Levy
Taxing districts set levies for schools, municipalities, counties, parks, libraries, fire, sanitary, and other local services.
Rate and extension
The county clerk extends rates against equalized assessed value; the assessor does not set the rate.
Bill and collection
The county treasurer bills and collects. Illinois taxes are commonly paid in installments after the tax year; confirm dates with the county treasurer.

Cook County versus the collar counties

Cook County classification

Cook County's elected assessor assigns each PIN a classification that determines its assessment level. The assessor says office, industrial, and retail buildings typically have a 25% level of assessment. Residential and multifamily differ, so use care with mixed-use and conversion assumptions.

Cook reassesses by region on a triennial cycle. The glossary definition of triennial reassessment is a reminder to check the current township calendar before relying on an old value.

Collar-county assessment

In DeKalb, DuPage, Grundy, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry, and Will, a township assessor commonly makes the initial assessment, reviewed by the county supervisor of assessments. The state guide explains the 33⅓% standard and equalization; county pages give local deadlines and forms.

Reassessment, appeals and deadlines

Appeals have strict deadlines; missing an assessor, Board of Review, or state filing date can defer the issue to a later tax year. Build a tax calendar during diligence.

Appeal forums

  1. Assessor review: informal or formal review at the assessor level, depending on the county and township calendar.
  2. County Board of Review: county-level appeal after assessment publication. In Cook, the Cook County Board of Review has its own filing rules.
  3. State or court review: after the county level, an owner may evaluate the Illinois Property Tax Appeal Board or circuit court with counsel.

Evidence for industrial property

Evidence may include income, comparable sales and recent arms-length transactions, vacancy or functional issues, rent-roll limits, deferred maintenance, environmental conditions, and constraints such as loading, clear height, sprinklers, power, or yard usability.

Cook County incentive classifications for industrial property

Cook County's Class 6b is the industrial incentive most often discussed. The assessor says it encourages new industrial facilities, rehabilitation of existing structures, and reuse of abandoned buildings.

Class 6b basics

The current Cook County Class 6b bulletin sets the reduced assessment at 10% for the first ten years, 15% in year eleven, and 20% in year twelve, subject to ordinance and application requirements. Review the application, municipal resolution, occupancy history, abandonment facts, renewal, and compliance, not just the shorthand.

Incentive diligence questions

Confirm approval, transferability, renewal, municipal resolution, compliance conditions, and lease allocation of benefit, refunds, and appeal control.

Enterprise zones and TIF districts

An enterprise zone is a designated area that may offer incentives if the project qualifies. Illinois DCEO says each zone has a local administrator and that a project seeking a building-material sales tax exemption certificate must contact that administrator. Eligibility is project-specific, not automatic.

A TIF district is different. A tax increment financing district can redirect eligible growth in tax revenue for public purposes within a district. It may appear on local maps, redevelopment agreements, or tax bills. In both cases, read the governing documents, expiration dates, and assignment rules.

Taxes in underwriting and leases

Lease controls

  • NNN leases: confirm whether real estate taxes pass through, whether reassessment is included, and whether refunds are shared.
  • Appeals: decide who may file, who pays professionals, and how settlements affect tenants.
  • Escrows: lenders may require tax escrows; buyers should align closing prorations and lender reserves.
  • Transfer taxes: Illinois, county, and municipal transfer taxes may apply. Chicago has its own finance department and rules. See transfer tax.

For sale-process context, see Illinois and Cook County considerations and pricing approach.

How can an Illinois owner manage property taxes year-round?

Keep a parcel file with every PIN, property card, area, use/classification, assessment notice, current and prior bill, lease, vacancy record, and capital-condition evidence. Correct factual errors with the responsible office. Appeals address assessments; the assessor neither sets each district's levy nor guarantees a bill.

Cook cycle versus filing windows

Cook reassesses each region every three years, but appeals and changed-property reviews also occur between triennial mailings. Use the current township calendar for notice and appeal dates; a town name is not the deadline. Track the Board of Review window separately. In collar counties, use county assessment publications, local Board rules, and annual equalization information, not Cook's cycle.

Assessor, Board of Review and PTAB are different steps

Start with local assessor review where available, then check county Board of Review filing rules. A PTAB appeal normally follows a Board decision; its filing instructions describe a 30-day period and decision/notification rules, including Cook-specific transmittal information. Have counsel confirm the trigger, entity representation, forms, evidence, and alternatives. PTAB recommends an appraisal for commercial and industrial appeals; asking prices alone are not equivalent evidence.

Calendar notice, filing, evidence, professional-engagement, decision, and further-review dates. An appeal does not necessarily suspend payments; confirm payment and refund handling with counsel and the treasurer.

Budget the bill and tenant allocation, not only an appeal result

Document a base case and higher-tax scenario for reassessment, equalization, levies, and incentive expiration. Do not count an unawarded reduction as savings. Track accrued taxes, installments, lender escrows, and closing prorations separately to avoid double-counting.

Check each lease's recoverable taxes, caps, appeal fees, refund sharing, and post-sale responsibility. A gross-lease owner may bear increases that a net-lease owner partly recovers; incentives may benefit tenants. Update the NOI bridge and cash budget.

Class 6b, Class 7 and Class 8: which program fits?

The Cook County Assessor's incentive directory supplies the governing eligibility bulletins and application forms. A reduced assessment classification is not a grant or an automatic percentage reduction of the tax bill. Confirm municipal support, project eligibility, deadlines, continued use, ownership changes and compliance before underwriting it.

Illinois owner tax questions

How can I lower commercial property taxes in Illinois?

Check parcel characteristics and assessment evidence, then file with the correct assessor or Board of Review within its calendar. Review further PTAB or court options with counsel. An assessment change does not guarantee a proportional tax-bill change because rates, levies and equalization also matter.

Does a Cook County incentive transfer automatically on sale?

Do not assume it. Verify approval documents, the remaining schedule, ownership/use requirements and current renewal or compliance rules with the Assessor and municipality before pricing the benefit.
