Federal and Illinois owner education

Commercial real estate taxes for retiring owners

Less management, larger deductions and lower lifetime taxes are different goals. Retiring industrial owners can compare passive 1031 strategies, rental loss limits, cost segregation, depreciation recapture and inheritance planning.

Reviewed October 8, 2026. Major federal and Illinois rules, not every statute, regulation, local ordinance or exception. Education, not personalized tax, legal, securities or retirement advice.

In brief

  • A lazy 1031 informally describes exchanging into less actively managed property, not an IRS program or guaranteed income.
  • Passive rental losses generally cannot offset wages, pensions, IRA distributions or Roth conversion income without an applicable exception.
  • Ordinary nonresidential buildings generally use 39 year straight line depreciation. Cost segregation changes eligible component timing, not land or the entire building.
  • Depreciation reduces basis and can change sale tax. Ordinary recapture, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain and other gain are distinct.
  • Federal and Illinois estate taxes are separate from income tax on an inheritance. Illinois's $4 million exclusion differs from the 2026 federal $15 million basic exclusion.
  • Owner lens: Model usable deductions, withdrawable cash and payable estate taxes. Paper losses and deferred gains are not household liquidity.

Which federal and Illinois tax rules surround commercial real estate?

Individuals, partnerships, S corporations, C corporations, trusts, estates and retirement accounts have different tax treatment. An LLC is a legal structure, not a universal federal tax classification. Separate property cash flow, income tax and estate exposure.

Properly classified interest, taxes, insurance, management and repairs may be rental expenses; loan principal and reserve deposits are not automatically deductible. Betterments, restorations and adaptations generally require capitalization. See the tangible property regulations and budget and capital guide. Federal personal SALT limits do not automatically govern rental business taxes.

Section 163(j) can limit business interest deductions. Small business exceptions depend on gross receipts and tax shelter rules, not building size. A real property trade or business can elect out but must use ADS for specified property. Review depreciation and bonus eligibility consequences before electing.

Section 199A continues after 2025 under Public Law 119-21: eligible noncorporate taxpayers may deduct up to 20% of QBI, subject to limits. Capital gains, wages and pensions are not QBI. Rental business qualification differs from passive classification. The rental safe harbor requires books, records, service hours and statements. Separate specialist review is needed for foreign owners/FIRPTA withholding, Opportunity Zones, development inventory, debt cancellation, distressed loans, conservation or charitable gifts, energy incentives, partnership basis elections and multiple state returns. These issues are flagged, not fully covered. See Publication 544, Section 163(j) guidance and IRS QBI guidance.

What is a lazy 1031 strategy, and is it appropriate for retirement?

Lazy 1031 informally means a qualifying exchange into less actively managed property. Not an IRS exemption or retirement plan, it assures no universal passive tax treatment. Options include eligible Delaware statutory trust (DST) interests, properly structured tenancy in common or professionally managed direct property, including net leased buildings.

Gain deferral and less landlord work can bring less control, fees, concentrated credit exposure, restricted resale and sponsor dependence. Alternatively, sell taxably and buy a diversified liquid portfolio. Compare after tax cash, household needs and risk, not just current deferral.

DST eligibility is specific, not automatic

Revenue Ruling 2004-86 conditionally treats DST interests as direct Section 1031 real estate; not every trust qualifies. Trustee restrictions on new capital, debt, leases and reinvestment can hamper tenant failure or major repair responses. Have independent counsel verify the offering and tax opinion.

DST offerings are commonly securities; eligibility and distribution requirements differ from tax eligibility. Review offering documents, debt allocations, sponsor compensation, conflicts, reserves, fees, condition, lease credit, distribution sources, exits and transfer restrictions. Projected distributions are not guaranteed yields; returned capital and borrowed cash are not earned profit.

Plan the exchange before closing

  1. Ask your CPA to calculate basis, projected gain, depreciation categories, suspended losses and state consequences of selling, exchanging or holding.
  2. Engage a qualified intermediary before relinquished property closes. Avoid actual or constructive receipt of proceeds; confirm same taxpayer and ownership structure requirements.
  3. Identify qualifying replacements in writing within 45 days of transfer. Follow the three property rule, 200% aggregate value rule or applicable restrictive 95% receipt exception.
  4. Receive replacements by the earlier of 180 days after transfer or the return due date, including extensions. Extending the return may be necessary; 180 days is not guaranteed.
  5. Test replacement value, equity, liabilities, closing costs and cash or other property received with advisers. Replacing mortgage debt does not require an identical new loan.
  6. Keep exchange documents, deferred gain and replacement basis/depreciation schedules; report on Form 8824.

Cash, other nonqualifying property and certain net liability relief can create taxable boot. Added cash can affect liability calculations. Qualifying real property generally must be held for business or investment, not primarily for sale or personal use. Related party, partnership and subsequent transfer plans need special review. See Form 8824 instructions.

A replacement purchase does not erase deferred gain

Replacement basis generally carries deferred gain forward. Depreciation separates exchanged basis from excess new investment; the new price does not fully reset depreciation basis. Later taxable sales may expose preserved gain and depreciation related tax. Unlike a fully taxable disposition, a 1031 generally does not release all suspended passive losses.

Ordinary REIT shares and partnership interests are not qualifying replacements. Section 721 UPREIT operating partnership contributions separately face debt, disguised sale and partnership rules; later REIT share conversion can change liquidity and tax. A 1031 to 721 sequence does not interchangeably exchange into public shares. Each step needs advice, not blanket tax free treatment.

Not all cost segregated assets qualify as Section 1031 real property. Exchange eligibility and recapture classification are separate; an exchange may not defer every component's ordinary recapture. It is not an IRA rollover. See retirement exit planning and Publication 541 for entity interests.

Can passive real estate losses offset active income in retirement?

Rental real estate is generally passive under Section 469 despite owner involvement. Passive losses ordinarily offset passive activity income, not wages, pensions, IRA withdrawals, Roth conversions or portfolio interest and dividends. Property sales have special character and loss release rules; not every capital gain qualifies as an offset.

Cash flow does not determine tax classification: passive rentals can produce cash and depreciation losses, even for retirees without jobs. Active participation, material participation, real estate professional status, QBI eligibility and retirement contribution compensation are distinct tests.

Active participation and the limited rental loss allowance

Qualifying active participants in rental decisions generally owning at least 10% may offset up to $25,000 of nonpassive income. For most taxpayers other than married filing separately, it generally phases out at $100,000 to $150,000 of specially defined MAGI. Separate filing rules can eliminate it for spouses living together. Pensions, distributions and conversions can affect MAGI; age or absent wages alone does not qualify.

Real estate professional status plus material participation

On a joint return, one spouse generally must exceed 750 hours in qualifying real property trades or businesses in which they materially participate, and half their annual personal services across all trades or businesses. Rentals separately require material participation, generally individually unless validly grouped by election.

Spousal hours can count for material participation, but cannot simply combine for one spouse's professional status thresholds. Employee hours have additional ownership conditions; investment monitoring alone is not qualifying service. Document activity and time. Retirement, a license or LLC management does not automatically qualify.

Other limits and the self rental trap

Owner basis and at risk limits precede passive limits; excess business loss limits can also defer deductions. Nonpassive rental losses are not automatically fully deductible. Track suspended amounts by limitation and activity.

Self rental rules generally make net rent from a business in which you materially participate nonpassive, not automatically its rental losses. Retaining the building after a business sale can change facts, especially with continued employment or involvement. Review grouping, leases and ownership before offsets.

Suspended passive losses generally become available on a fully taxable disposition of the entire activity interest to an unrelated person, subject to grouping and installment rules. A 1031 ordinarily is not that exit. At death, losses generally are deductible only above the relevant inherited basis increase, not transferred to heirs. See IRS Publication 925.

Illustrative loss offset, not tax savings promised

Assume $50,000 pension/IRA income, $20,000 passive rent from one activity and a $30,000 depreciation driven passive loss from another, sufficient basis and at risk amounts, and no other adjustments. The loss may offset $20,000 passive income; $10,000 carries forward unless an allowance or exception permits use. It does not automatically eliminate $30,000 pension/IRA income. Portfolio dividends would not qualify for the ordinary passive offset.

How does straight line depreciation work for an industrial building?

Ordinary nonresidential real property generally uses 39 year straight line depreciation with the mid month convention under the general depreciation system. Land is not depreciable; residential rentals, qualifying improvements, short life assets, ADS and specialized property can differ. Start when ready and available for intended use, not contract signing.

Allocate acquisition and capitalized transaction costs to land, building and supported components. Improvements increase basis; allowed or allowable depreciation generally reduces it even if unclaimed. Revocable trust or disregarded LLC transfers normally reset neither basis nor depreciation.

Illustration: $1,950,000 building basis excluding land and separately classified components yields $50,000 per full ordinary year of 39 year straight line depreciation. First and last years use the mid month convention, not a universal $50,000 acquisition year deduction. This recommends neither appraisal allocation nor tax election.

Rent remains potentially taxable after building depreciation ends. New eligible improvements have separate schedules. Principal repayment, reserve transfers, depreciation and operating cash differ. See Publication 946 and Publication 551.

Can cost segregation and bonus depreciation improve a retiree's tax position?

Supported cost segregation separates eligible shorter life assets from building structure: potentially certain 5 year equipment, 7 year furnishings and 15 year land improvements. It neither depreciates land nor changes total supported purchase basis or classifies every industrial feature as short life.

Review engineering support, invoices, plans, allocations, component definitions and existing tax schedule reconciliation. Older acquisitions may require an accounting method change, Form 3115 and a Section 481(a) adjustment, not a full current deduction. A 2026 study does not reset acquisition or placed in service dates.

Current bonus rules are not the old phase down

Public Law 119-21 restored 100% additional first year depreciation for qualifying property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025, subject to statutory and acquisition rules. Binding contracts, self construction, related parties, prior use and elections matter. Do not use old percentage schedules or promise a full warehouse write off.

Eligible short life property and qualifying improvement property may qualify under Section 168(k); ordinary 39 year buildings and land generally do not. Qualified improvement property generally covers eligible interior improvements made by the taxpayer after a nonresidential building first enters service. Enlargement, elevators, escalators and internal structural framework are excluded. Verify classification and eligibility.

The 2025 law's Section 168(n) separately covers certain qualified production property, subject to manufacturing or production use, construction or acquisition, timing, election and recapture requirements. Rental warehouses, offices and industrial acquisitions do not qualify universally; tenant manufacturing alone does not qualify ordinary landlords. Seek specialized review of Notice 2026-16's related ownership exceptions and requirements.

See IRS Notice 2026-11 guidance and Publication 946. Law can change; use the actual asset and tax year's rules.

Section 179 is a different election

Section 179 may cover eligible purchased business assets and elected real property improvements: eligible QIP and specified roofs, HVAC, fire protection, alarm and security systems. Annual limits, investment phaseouts, taxable business income limits, business use conditions and special lessor restrictions apply. It neither automatically expenses land or buildings nor equals bonus depreciation.

Timing benefits can fail to become current savings

Compare study cost, usable deductions, suspended losses, hold period, recapture, federal/Illinois differences and future brackets. Without passive income or a usable exception, a passive retiree may only increase suspended losses. Near term sales can shorten timing benefits; Illinois bonus modifications can delay state deductions. Cost segregation does not increase NOI or fund repairs.

A lower income retirement year is not automatically best for a large deduction. Ask a CPA to compare claiming, electing out where permitted, improving and selling, considering losses, QBI, NIIT and benefits. Use the IRS Cost Segregation audit guide directory, not vendor savings percentages.

What taxes arise on a sale, and what does depreciation recapture mean?

Calculate gain from amount realized after applicable selling costs less adjusted basis, not cash after mortgage payoff. Debt payoff ordinarily changes cash, not property gain. Support land, building and separate asset allocations.

Accelerated components, QIP, older assets and corporate owners need separate analysis. Section 291 can add ordinary corporate recapture. CRE has no universal recapture rate.

Worked sale example: gain is not equity cash

Assume an individual pays $2,000,000: $500,000 land and $1,500,000 ordinary building. With $300,000 straight line building depreciation, no other basis changes, a $2,600,000 sale and $100,000 eligible selling costs, adjusted basis is $1,700,000. Net amount realized is $2,500,000; gain is $800,000.

A $900,000 mortgage payoff leaves $1,600,000 cash before income tax, but does not reduce the $800,000 gain. Assuming sufficient building gain, no ordinary recapture and no relevant netting or lookback adjustments, up to $300,000 may be unrecaptured Section 1250 gain and $500,000 other qualifying gain. Actual allocation and the return control, not one rate on $800,000.

Calculate NIIT, Illinois taxes, suspended loss use and household income. Installment sales generally do not defer depreciation recapture despite later cash receipts; seller financing adds buyer credit and liquidity risk. Qualifying exchanges can defer eligible gain; partial exchanges may recognize some. See Publication 544, Form 4797 instructions and capital gain rate guidance.

How do Illinois income and property taxes differ from federal taxes?

Illinois individuals generally pay 4.95% of net income under current law, with no preferential long term gain rate. Corporations and certain pass through entities or trusts have different income/replacement tax rules. Partnerships, S corporations and trusts generally face 1.5% replacement tax; non-S corporations generally face 7% income tax plus 2.5% replacement tax, subject to rules and exemptions. Review elections and classifications; LLC taxes differ.

Resident and nonresident sourcing and credits differ. Illinois property can generate Illinois source rent and gain after its owner moves. A qualifying federal exchange generally defers gain entering the state starting point; track Illinois modifications and historical basis differences. See Illinois income and replacement tax rates and Schedule NR guidance.

Federal bonus is not automatically the Illinois deduction

Illinois calculates federal bonus depreciation additions and subtractions on IL-4562, carried to the appropriate return or Schedule M. Keep separate federal/Illinois schedules through ownership, sale and exchange. Current instructions also address Section 168(n) for tax years beginning January 1, 2026. Federal first year deductions multiplied by 4.95% are not necessarily immediate state savings.

Use current IL-4562 instructions for the year and asset. Reversals and disposition adjustments can matter years after a study or purchase.

Property, transfer and retirement income taxes are separate

Illinois property taxes depend on assessment, equalization, levies and local extension/collection, not federal depreciation. Cost segregation or low taxable rental profit does not automatically lower the bill. Review Cook County incentive classifications, renewal/compliance and appeal deadlines by parcel. See the Illinois industrial property tax guide.

State, county and municipal transfer taxes can apply, including Chicago requirements. Confirm situs, exemptions, declarations, covered entity interest transfers and legal and contractual payment responsibility. A federal 1031 is not a blanket transfer tax exemption. Sales, use, lease, withholding or business taxes may cover other assets or activities in mixed transactions.

Eligible federally taxed retirement plan/IRA distributions and certain other retirement income may qualify for Illinois's subtraction in Publication 120. Retirement does not turn ordinary rent or directly realized property gain into qualifying distributions.

Is there inheritance tax, estate tax or both on a retiree's real estate?

Ordinary domestic estates face federal estate tax, not general inheritance tax on recipients. Illinois currently has estate tax, not separate inheritance tax. Estate tax concerns transfers at death; inherited IRA withdrawals, later rent and sales can still incur income tax. Other states, noncitizen/nonresident estates, covered expatriate transfers and treaty rules may differ.

Federal estate and gift rules in 2026

Public Law 119-21 increased the federal estate and gift basic exclusion to $15 million per individual for 2026, not per building, with later inflation adjustments under current law rather than an automatic 2026 cut. Prior taxable gifts can consume it; gross estate, adjusted gifts, debts, deductions, ownership and valuation affect filing and tax.

A surviving spouse may elect portability of unused federal exclusion, generally on Form 706. It neither automatically doubles exclusion nor transfers to children or ports the GST exemption. Counsel must review marital deductions, citizenship conditions, trusts and prior gifts. Federal estate returns are generally due nine months after death; review extensions and portability relief separately.

See IRS estate and gift updates and Form 706 instructions. State filings or portability elections can matter without federal estate tax due.

Illinois's separate $4 million exclusion and no portability

Illinois's separate $4 million estate tax exclusion has no spousal portability under current law. The survivor does not automatically get $8 million. Estates below the federal exclusion may still need an Illinois return and owe state estate tax.

This exclusion is a threshold in an interrelated computation, not a $4 million deduction followed by a flat rate. Use the Attorney General's instructions and calculator. Nonresident exposure depends on gross estate, adjusted taxable gifts, deductions, Illinois situs and apportionment, not just the Illinois building's value.

The Attorney General administers Illinois Form 700 and estate tax information. Illinois situs real property can expose estates of decedents living elsewhere. Returns and payment are generally due nine months after death; filing extensions do not automatically extend payment. Confirm requirements with counsel and the instruction fact sheet.

Estate value can exceed available cash

Count property and entity interests, accounts, other assets and includible life insurance, not just checking accounts. Allowed debts may reduce taxable estate, but do not eliminate loan payments or filing analysis. Do not double count a building and its holding entity. Support appraisals and entity valuations; discounts are not automatic.

Plan cash for taxes, loan covenants, administration and carrying costs while heirs choose to hold or sell. Trusts can address succession and marital/state planning; revocable living trusts alone do not erase estate tax. Have counsel evaluate federal/state marital elections, including appropriate QTIP, not generic bypass trust or gifting rules.

What happens to basis, depreciation and suspended losses at death?

Inherited property generally receives fair market value basis at death or a permitted alternate valuation, subject to statutory exceptions. This can step basis up or down; estate tax payment alone does not determine eligibility. Support the valuation and identify qualifying property or interests.

For directly inherited commercial property, allocate supported new basis to land and depreciable assets. Apply the heir's depreciation rules and service timing, not blindly the decedent's schedule. Inherited partnership or corporate interests do not automatically reset building basis; partnership Section 754/743 adjustments and entity rules can be critical.

Inherited building example, not an estate tax calculation

Assume directly owned building and land with $1,700,000 adjusted basis qualifies for $2,800,000 date of death basis. A later $2,900,000 sale with $100,000 selling costs, no intervening depreciation/improvements or other adjustments leaves $2,800,000 net amount realized and inherited basis: zero illustrative gain. Estate tax could still have been due; post-inheritance depreciation changes later basis.

Not every 1031 chain or trust eliminates tax. Qualifying real property held at death may receive Section 1014 basis adjustment; review account holdings, entity structure, prior transfers and estate inclusion. Keeping lifetime taxable sale proceeds until death does not undo realized gain or sale tax.

Gifting and inherited retirement accounts are different

Lifetime gifts generally carry donor basis for gain, with special loss basis and other adjustments, not automatic market value basis. Debt transfers can cause part sale treatment or other tax. Gifts can require Form 709 and use exclusion without immediate gift tax. Annual gift exclusion is neither a per building reset nor the lifetime exclusion.

Income in respect of a decedent (IRD), including taxable inherited traditional IRA amounts, gets no ordinary Section 1014 basis adjustment. Death does not make IRA real estate tax free. Review beneficiary distributions, account basis and possible deductions for estate tax attributable to IRD.

At death, suspended passive losses generally are allowed only above the relevant basis increase, not passed intact to heirs. Other suspended losses, gifts and entity losses have separate rules. Keep separate schedules for CPA reconciliation to the final return. See Publication 551, Publication 559 and Publication 925.

How do property taxes interact with retirement withdrawals and benefits?

Property and retirement account income are not interchangeable. IRA/plan property provides no personal depreciation deductions. Prohibited transaction and unrelated business income rules can apply even to Roth accounts. See the retirement account and CRE guide for ownership, contribution eligibility and RMD liquidity.

Net investment income tax is separate from depreciation recapture

NIIT generally equals 3.8% of the lesser of net investment income or MAGI above $200,000 single/head of household, $250,000 married filing jointly/qualifying surviving spouse or $125,000 married filing separately. Rent and investment gains commonly count. Qualifying nonpassive business income may be excluded; real estate professional status alone is no blanket exemption.

Retirement distributions and Roth conversions generally are not net investment income but can raise MAGI and NIIT on other investment income. See IRS NIIT guidance and Form 8960 instructions.

Medicare IRMAA and Social Security tax

Sale gains, conversions and larger taxable withdrawals can affect Medicare Part B and prescription premiums separately from income tax. SSA generally uses tax information from two years earlier; IRMAA MAGI is AGI plus tax exempt interest. Qualifying life changing events with income reductions can support reconsideration; property sales alone do not automatically qualify. See SSA's IRMAA rules.

Property income can affect federal combined income for Social Security tax. Up to 85% of benefits can be taxable income, not taxed at 85%. Taxability differs from the pre-full-retirement-age earnings test. Ordinary rent is not automatically wages; activity and service exceptions matter. Consult Publication 915 and SSA; benefit effects are not one tax.

Illiquid real estate still faces RMD and inherited account payout deadlines. Most noneligible designated beneficiaries have ten years, with annual distributions required in some cases. Qualified Roth inheritance rules differ from traditional accounts but disallow indefinite holding. A 1031 does not satisfy an RMD.

What should a retiring owner bring to tax and estate advisers?

  1. Ownership and family map: deeds, entities, operating agreements, trust documents, beneficiaries, citizenship/residence and authority to act.
  2. Tax basis file: closing statements, land/building allocations, capital improvements, depreciation, cost segregation and federal versus Illinois schedules.
  3. Loss and participation records: passive carryovers, basis/at risk limits, prior Section 1231 losses, elections and documented hours.
  4. Exchange and debt records: deferred gains, Form 8824 history, intermediary proposals, loan balances, recourse, prepayment and entity consents.
  5. Property cash needs: rent roll, leases, expense recoveries, management proposal, capital plan, vacancy case and sale estimates.
  6. Household tax and benefits: filing status, projected pension/IRA income, conversions, RMDs, Medicare and Social Security information.
  7. Estate liquidity: valuations, prior taxable gifts, federal portability filings, Illinois exposure and cash available for taxes and administration.

Compare holding, taxable sale, exchange, cost segregation study and lifetime versus inheritance transfers. Project usable deductions, replacement basis, management/control tradeoffs, fees, family goals and adverse cash cases. Deferral is only one retirement factor. This guide recommends no DST, exchange, trust or security.

Questions retirees holding real estate ask

What is a lazy 1031 exchange?

An informal term for exchanging qualifying property into less actively managed investments, such as eligible DSTs or professionally managed net leased property. It is not an IRS program, income guarantee or waiver of normal 1031 requirements.

Can a DST replace my industrial building when I retire?

Some DST interests qualify as replacement real property under specific tax conditions. Have independent advisers review structure, offering documents, debt, fees, sponsor, property risks and transfer restrictions. Less management does not mean low risk or ready cash.

Can I do a 1031 exchange directly into REIT shares?

No. Ordinary REIT shares are not qualifying replacements. Section 721 contributions to UPREIT partnerships are separate transactions, not interchangeable 1031 exchanges. Multistep sequences need separate legal and tax analysis.

Can I keep some sale cash for living expenses and exchange the rest?

Possibly. Cash or other nonqualifying property received can create taxable boot and reduce deferral. Debt and expense allocations matter. Have the CPA and intermediary calculate tax and available cash before closing.

Do I always get 180 days to complete an exchange?

No. Generally, identify replacements within 45 days and receive them by the earlier of 180 days or the tax return due date, including extensions. Arrange the intermediary and any needed return extension before deadlines.

Does my 1031 replacement get a full new depreciation basis?

Not automatically. Deferred gain generally carries into replacement basis; exchanged basis and excess investment have separate depreciation rules. A new price neither erases deferred gain nor guarantees full-price deductions.

Can rental losses offset my pension or IRA withdrawals?

Generally not for passive losses without an applicable allowance or exception. Pension/IRA income is not passive activity income. Real estate professional status plus material participation and qualifying rental allowances have separate tests.

Can cost segregation wipe out the tax on a Roth conversion?

Not automatically. Passive depreciation losses generally cannot offset conversions, which can raise MAGI and reduce rental loss allowances. Basis, at risk and other limits may also block deductions. Model usable losses, not total study deductions.

Are dividends passive income that rental losses can offset?

Portfolio dividends and interest generally are not Section 469 passive activity income. Income requiring little work may be “passive” colloquially without qualifying for rental loss offsets.

Does retirement make me a real estate professional?

No. Annual qualifying services generally must exceed 750 hours and half your personal services; rentals separately require material participation as applicable. Retirement, licensing or LLC ownership is not enough.

Can a retiree use the $25,000 rental loss allowance?

Possibly, with qualifying active participation, ownership and MAGI. For most taxpayers other than married filing separately, it generally phases out at $100,000 to $150,000 of specially defined MAGI. Retirement alone does not qualify.

Can rent from my own business absorb my other passive rental losses?

Not necessarily. Self rental rules generally make net rent from a business in which you materially participate nonpassive, but do not automatically make rental losses nonpassive. Review ownership, participation and grouping before using offsets.

What happens to suspended rental losses when I sell?

Fully taxable dispositions of the entire activity interest to unrelated persons generally release passive losses, subject to applicable rules. A 1031 generally is not that disposition; grouping, recognized income and installment timing can change results.

How many years do I depreciate a commercial building?

Generally 39 years, straight line with the mid month convention, for ordinary nonresidential real property under GDS. Land is excluded; ADS, eligible components, qualified improvements and special property can differ.

Can I avoid later tax by never claiming depreciation?

Generally no. Allowed or allowable depreciation reduces adjusted basis even if unclaimed. Have a CPA review records and correction procedures rather than deliberately skipping deductions.

Is cost segregation worthwhile near retirement?

Compare usable deductions, study cost, carryforwards, hold period, state differences and later recapture. Passive owners may only create suspended losses; near term sales can shorten timing benefits. It is not universally beneficial.

Can I write off my whole warehouse with 100% bonus depreciation?

No, not an ordinary warehouse purchase under Section 168(k). Eligible shorter life components and improvements may qualify under current rules; land and ordinary 39 year buildings generally do not. Review specialized production property rules separately.

Does a new cost segregation study make an old building eligible for new bonus rules?

No. Study dates do not reset acquisition or placed in service dates. Review eligible classifications, historical law and any accounting method change for the original assets.

Is all depreciation recapture taxed at 25%?

No. Section 1245 ordinary recapture, Section 1250 ordinary recapture and unrecaptured Section 1250 gain differ. The latter's maximum individual rate is 25%, not a mandatory flat rate. Classification, gain, netting and owner type matter.

Does paying off my mortgage reduce taxable sale gain?

Ordinary loan payoff reduces cash, not generally property gain. Gain depends on amount realized, applicable sale costs and adjusted basis. Loan principal is not tax basis.

Can seller financing defer depreciation recapture?

Recapture generally is recognized in the sale year even with installment treatment of other eligible gain. Seller financing adds buyer credit, interest and liquidity issues. Review the full transaction before claiming deferral.

Does Illinois allow the same immediate federal bonus deduction?

Not automatically. Illinois uses IL-4562 addition/subtraction modifications and related returns. Keep separate state/federal depreciation schedules; review current asset, year and disposition rules.

Does being retired exempt my Illinois rent or property sale gain?

No. Eligible retirement account distributions can qualify for an Illinois subtraction. Retirement does not turn ordinary rent or directly realized property gain into retirement plan income.

If I move away, do Illinois taxes on my building disappear?

No. Illinois source rent, gains and Illinois situs estate property can remain relevant for nonresidents. Have affected-state advisers review sourcing, residency, credits, entity treatment and estate apportionment.

Can a building sale raise my Medicare premiums?

Yes. Taxable income can affect IRMAA, generally based on a return two years earlier, separately from sale income tax. Qualifying life changing events may support reconsideration; sales alone do not automatically qualify.

Can IRA withdrawals raise net investment income tax on rent?

Yes. Retirement distributions generally are not net investment income but can raise MAGI, increasing rental/investment income subject to 3.8% NIIT under applicable activity and threshold rules.

Does Illinois have an inheritance tax on my children's building?

Illinois currently has estate tax, not separate inheritance tax. Estates may owe tax before distributions; heirs can owe income tax on later rent, sales or taxable inherited account withdrawals. Other states may differ.

What are the federal and Illinois estate exclusions in 2026?

Federal: $15 million per individual for 2026 under current law, affected by prior taxable gifts and other rules. Illinois: separate $4 million exclusion, no state portability. Building equity alone does not determine estate tax.

Does a married couple automatically get twice the Illinois estate exclusion?

No. Illinois has no portability of unused spousal exclusion. Federal portability requires a separate election, generally on Form 706. Estate planning, marital deductions and trust elections need individual legal review.

Will my children get a new tax basis for inherited property?

Qualifying inherited property generally gets fair market value basis at death or applicable alternate value, stepping up or down. Direct property, entity interests, trusts and retirement accounts differ; not every underlying asset gets new basis.

Should I gift my building now to avoid inheritance taxes?

There is no universal answer. Lifetime gifts generally carry donor basis for gain and can use gift exclusion or require returns; debt and control changes add issues. Compare estate exposure, income tax basis, family goals and liquidity with counsel before transferring.

Does an inherited IRA building get the same step up as personally held property?

No ordinary inherited property basis adjustment applies to taxable traditional IRA distributions, which are income in respect of a decedent. Analyze account holdings, basis and beneficiary distribution rules separately.

Do unused passive rental losses pass to my heirs?

Not automatically. At death, suspended passive losses generally are deductible only above the relevant inherited basis increase, not passed on as heir carryforwards. Review the final return and other loss limits.

Does putting the building into a living trust remove estate tax?

Revocable living trusts generally neither remove property from the owner's taxable estate nor reset lifetime basis. They can help administration and succession. Tax results depend on trust, ownership, powers and estate rules.

Is exchange and hold until death always the best retirement strategy?

No. Basis treatment at death is one factor. Liquidity, tenant/sponsor risk, management, fees, estate tax, family preferences and future law can outweigh deferral. Compare taxable sales and other choices; inheritance does not guarantee tax elimination.
