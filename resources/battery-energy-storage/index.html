Battery energy storage systems: an industrial owner's guide

A BESS stores electricity for later use. Its value to a warehouse, manufacturer or landowner depends on the meter, load profile, utility rules, safety design and contract, not battery size alone. This guide covers operation, purchases and storage leases.

Reviewed October 8, 2026. General property education, not engineering design, emergency instructions, an investment recommendation or a guarantee of approvals or savings.

In brief

  • BESS means battery energy storage system, sometimes written B.E.S.S. It stores energy; it does not create free electricity.
  • kW describes power; kWh describes stored energy. Both matter, along with usable capacity and losses.
  • Reducing a short billing peak is different from moving energy between hours or backing up a building.
  • A grid connected battery does not automatically work during an outage. Safe islanded operation and critical loads require specific engineered capability.
  • Utility approval, zoning and fire approval are separate. Export permissions and tariff economics must be confirmed.
  • Owner lens: Determine who pays the capital cost, who receives the meter savings and who owes removal and restoration. Tenant savings do not automatically become landlord NOI.

How does a battery energy storage system work?

A BESS stores electricity and releases it on command. Grid or onsite generation charges the batteries; conversion and auxiliary equipment consume some energy. Many commercial systems use lithium ion, but chemistry, equipment, performance and hazards vary.

The battery cabinet is only one part of the system

The converter supplies permitted loads or authorized exports. The EMS sets charge/discharge timing and output within battery, tariff and project limits; the BMS monitors battery conditions. Neither replaces a building fire alarm or utility export permission.

Solar and storage can connect on the AC or DC side. An engineer must verify compatibility, controls, protection and metering; batteries are not universally compatible with existing solar. This guide provides no wiring, installation or emergency procedures.

What is the difference between kW, kWh and storage duration?

kW measures delivery rate; kWh measures energy. Power alone does not establish duration. At larger scales, 1 MW equals 1,000 kW and 1 MWh equals 1,000 kWh.

Illustration: 1,000 kWh of delivered energy at a constant 250 kW lasts four hours ideally. With only 800 kWh available after reserves and capacity limits, duration is 3.2 hours before any losses outside that measurement boundary. Actual runtime varies; usable battery DC energy is not the same as delivered AC energy.

Specify nominal versus usable capacity, beginning versus end of life, and measurement at cells, inverter or meter. Include temperature, HVAC/control loads, efficiency, cycling, standby time, reserves and degradation. Temperature, equipment ratings and state of charge can also limit power.

Round trip efficiency compares returned and charging energy at defined boundaries; batteries return less than they take in. Aging and cycling generally reduce capacity. Warranties can limit years, throughput, cycles, temperature and operating conditions. Replacement or augmentation may require approvals, downtime and disposal planning.

What can BESS do for an industrial building?

Reduce a billing peak, if the tariff rewards it

Peak shaving discharges during chargeable demand peaks, such as manufacturing starts, refrigeration, compressors or synchronized charging. Billing intervals, demand ratchets, peak windows and charges determine the saving. Recharging can create a new peak.

Shift energy between hours

Charging cheaply and discharging later can save money when the rate spread exceeds losses, degradation and other costs. A flat tariff provides no automatic time arbitrage benefit. Wholesale prices may differ from tenant retail rates; export credits and incentives may differ from import prices.

Use more onsite solar or manage export

Metering and controls determine whether storage can move eligible solar output to later loads. Compare self consumption with permitted export compensation; storing every excess kWh may not pay. Batteries and solar can operate independently, subject to design and utility rules.

Support selected loads during an outage, only when designed for it

Grid tied operation does not automatically provide backup. It requires engineered utility isolation, suitable inverters/controls, protection, switching and approved islanding. Specify critical loads, starting surges, outage duration, reserves and restart behavior. Battery size alone does not ensure enough instantaneous or motor starting power for the facility.

Backup reserves reduce energy available for bill savings. Study generator/solar/battery integration separately. Emergency and legally required standby loads must meet applicable rules; a vendor's backup label does not replace required systems. Follow approved procedures, never improvised reconnection.

Provide grid services under a separate agreement

Approved storage may serve demand response or other utility/market programs. Check enrollment, dispatch, telemetry, penalties and revenue. Multiple projections can double count the same power, energy or hours; confirm compatible uses and performance risk allocation.

Is an onsite battery the same as leasing land for grid storage?

Two property opportunities with different economics
ModelPrimary purposeProperty owner diligence
Behind the meter onsite BESSServe the facility account, manage bills or support engineered resilienceInterval data, tenant meter rights, tariff, equipment space, financing and savings allocation
Front of the meter grid projectStore and deliver energy for grid/market services under applicable permissionsDeveloper credit, transmission/distribution access, permitting, options, land rent, easements and decommissioning

Grid project income follows project contracts; landlords typically receive land rent, not automatically market profit or cheaper building power. Approved onsite projects may also export. Neither label settles interconnection or revenue rules.

A nearby substation, large service or vacant yard does not establish capacity or approval. Feasibility, upgrades, queues, zoning, access and community/safety constraints can block projects. Set milestones, payments and termination rights so a long, inexpensive option cannot tie up leasing, truck circulation or sale.

How should an owner evaluate battery savings and costs?

Collect a representative year of bills and interval data, more if seasons or operations changed. Verify supply/delivery tariffs, meter ownership, billing intervals, ratchets, import/export arrangements and demand response contracts. Compare storage with efficiency, controls and load scheduling before purchase.

Hypothetical example, not a ComEd quote: at $15 per kW per month, reliably reducing chargeable demand by 100 kW in each of 12 months saves $18,000 annually. Reducing one isolated peak does not establish that monthly result.

Subtract $4,000 charging/auxiliary energy, $3,000 service/software and $2,000 replacement reserve funding: annual cash benefit before financing and tax is $9,000. A $180,000 net installed cost, with no incentive, gives 20 years simple payback. These invented assumptions are not a proposal, service life or recommendation.

Model degradation, replacement timing, discounting, tariffs, availability, contract term, financing, taxes and residual/removal costs. Reserve funding is cash planning, not automatically a deduction; avoid double counting funded replacement. Value resilience separately through a documented interruption scenario, not a bill credit.

Require itemized civil works, electrical upgrades, engineering, permitting, equipment, commissioning, software, communications, safety work, maintenance, insurance and end of life costs. Identify warranty exclusions, spare parts and response obligations. Incentives and federal clean electricity/storage credits are project and taxpayer dependent; ask tax counsel about current eligibility, ownership, timing, labor, sourcing, elections and recapture rather than assuming a quoted percentage. The IRS Section 48E overview is a starting point, not confirmation that your project qualifies.

Tenants paying utilities may receive the savings. Gross leases can reduce landlord bills; net leases can also reduce reimbursements. Neither gross savings nor equipment cost automatically equals the NOI effect. See NOI, DCF and energy cost controls.

What utility and permit questions apply in Illinois?

Identify the serving utility and local jurisdiction; ComEd does not serve all Illinois properties. PJM/MISO processes depend on connection location and type. Retail utility approval and regional market participation are separate.

  1. Utility screen: confirm voltage, service, transformer, proposed power, charging source, export/nonexport mode, metering and required application/studies. Do not assume nonexport removes all interconnection requirements.
  2. Site and title screen: review zoning, permitted use, setbacks, easements, flood/drainage, access, loading, fire apparatus routes and landlord/lender rights.
  3. Code and fire screen: ask the authority having jurisdiction which editions, amendments, permits, studies and submittals apply to this project.
  4. Financial screen: identify study deposits, upgrade costs, procurement conditions, deadlines, tariff permissions and who owns equipment or improvements.
  5. Operating screen: establish dispatch, inspections, communications, emergency planning, utility authorization and ongoing compliance before operation.

Use the serving utility's official channels and Illinois Commerce Commission information with an experienced project engineer. For ComEd accounts, review its DER interconnection guidelines and current rates and tariffs, confirming any updates directly. Not every application falls into the same interconnection category. Export eligibility, participation and deadlines must be confirmed under the current rules; a submitted application or queue position is not an operating approval.

For standalone/wholesale projects, confirm applicable PJM or MISO and utility processes. Nearby transmission does not guarantee connection. Zoning does not supply capacity; utility approval does not waive building/fire requirements.

What safety and siting issues should an industrial owner understand?

Electrical equipment and battery chemistries present fire, electrical, thermal and chemical hazards. Lithium ion thermal runaway can spread between cells, release heat and flammable/toxic gases, and reignite. Address credible hazards in design and emergency plans; listings or outdoor locations do not eliminate risk.

UL 9540 certifies system equipment safety; UL 9540A tests thermal runaway fire propagation. A test report is not a universal safety pass or site permit. Professionals must review the complete system, installation and matching test evidence. Edition 6 installation level testing introduces pass/fail criteria effective January 1, 2027; criteria differ by edition and level. See UL's explanation.

NFPA 855 and fire/building/electrical requirements depend on locally adopted editions, amendments, equipment and installation. Confirm documentation, location, separation, access, detection/protection, emergency plans and hazard analysis with officials. No universal setback, quantity exemption or suppression method is supplied here.

Preserve circulation, exits and fire access. Review trucks, flooding, drainage, cooling noise, neighbors, ventilation/gas hazards, foundations, security and signs. Indoor, outdoor, rooftop and container installations have distinct requirements; no location is universally best.

Coordinate insurer approval and fire department review before committing. Provide operator training, inspections, authorized shutdown/response procedures and vendor contacts. Do not open cabinets, bypass interlocks or attempt electrical repairs. This is not incident guidance; follow site procedures and emergency services.

Use qualified removal, transport and recycling providers under applicable waste, transport and environmental rules; damaged batteries may need special handling. EPA says most discarded lithium ion batteries likely are hazardous waste due to ignitability or reactivity, not ordinary trash. Review its commercial recycling guidance. Contracts must assign cleanup, restoration, financial security and surviving obligations; warranties are not decommissioning funds.

What belongs in a battery lease or ownership agreement?

Energy and property counsel should distinguish purchase, equipment/service, tenant improvement and ground lease rights. Identify the equipment owner, operator and utility account holder; the installer does not necessarily hold every responsibility.

  • Site rights: equipment footprint, access, cables, drainage, utility easements, maintenance areas and restoration; preserve truck courts and expansion plans.
  • Economics: fixed/variable payments, development option rent, milestones, savings measurement, baselines, escalation, charges and dispatch obligations.
  • Approvals and operations: permit/interconnection responsibility, costs, commissioning, inspections, data access, remote control security, downtime and notices.
  • Risk allocation: insurance, indemnities, pollution/cleanup duties, casualty, neighbor impacts, defaults, parent support and required security.
  • Transfers and endings: lender consent, assignment, sale, tenant expiration, equipment liens, casualty termination, removal deadlines and decommissioning security.

Check fire/property, interruption, liability and environmental coverage and exclusions; standard landlord policies may not suffice. Confirm lender liens, collateral, consent and remedies. Non disturbance/access agreements must protect existing lease and lender rights.

BESS does not automatically raise appraised value. Buyers can discount short warranties, unfavorable contracts, restricted land, contingent revenue or removal costs. Long storage leases can support income while reducing flexibility. For retirement, assess developer credit, operations and future marketability, not rent alone.

An owner checklist from proposal through end of life

  1. Define the objective: bill reduction, solar use, critical load resilience or land rent, with measurable priorities and no conflicting assumptions.
  2. Establish the baseline: bills, interval data, operations, outages, tenant/meter rights and equipment/service constraints.
  3. Screen the site: title, leases, lender, utility, engineer, fire officials, insurer and zoning before equipment orders.
  4. Compare proposals: usable delivered power/energy, end of life performance, system certification/test evidence, full costs and controls.
  5. Model adverse cases: smaller savings, changed tariff, tenant departure, degradation, downtime, delayed approvals and removal costs.
  6. Document contracts: guarantees and their conditions, measurable service levels, approved operating modes, data rights and security.
  7. Commission professionally: retain approved plans, permits, utility authorization, acceptance records, warranties and operating/emergency documents.
  8. Monitor and maintain: reconcile measured results with the baseline, maintain logs and support, and update plans when operations or tenants change.
  9. Plan the exit: transfers, lender rights, lease expiry, augmentation, recycling, removal and restoration funding.

Capacity is not feasibility. Retain a complete file for future owners, tenants, lenders and managers, independent of the salesperson.

20 questions industrial owners ask about BESS

What does B.E.S.S. stand for?

B.E.S.S., usually BESS, means battery energy storage system: batteries, power conversion, controls, thermal management, protection and site equipment that store and later deliver electricity.

Does a BESS generate electricity?

No. It stores permitted grid or onsite generation. Charging, conversion and auxiliary losses mean less energy is delivered than charged at comparable measurement boundaries.

What is the difference between kW and kWh?

kW measures power; kWh measures energy. Both instantaneous output and usable delivered energy must meet the load and duration. Nominal capacity alone does not establish runtime.

Do I need solar panels to use battery storage?

No. Systems may charge from the grid under utility/tariff rules. Adding solar requires project specific review of compatibility, controls, exports and economics.

Will a grid connected battery automatically power my warehouse during an outage?

No. Backup requires engineered utility isolation, suitable equipment/controls, approved protection and defined loads. Many bill management systems cannot island; whole building backup is not implicit.

Can a battery back up my entire manufacturing facility?

Only if the approved design supports its power, starting surges, duration and operations. An engineer must assess loads and outage goals; critical load backup may be more practical.

How does a BESS reduce demand charges?

Discharging during chargeable peaks can reduce grid demand if power, duration and controls match the tariff/load. Ratchets, peak windows and recharging can erase savings. Use interval data.

Does battery storage always lower electricity bills?

No. Tariffs, peaks, charging costs, losses, controls, degradation and project costs determine savings. Flat rates or unsuitable loads may offer little benefit. Compare supported simulations with cheaper efficiency/scheduling options.

How long does a commercial battery last?

Life varies with chemistry, temperature, cycling, throughput, operating limits and maintenance. Review capacity/service warranties, end of life performance, replacement costs and financial assumptions.

Can I sell stored electricity back to the grid?

Only with applicable permissions, interconnection, metering and commercial arrangements. Technical capability establishes neither export rights nor payment rates. Retail and wholesale processes differ.

Does a nonexport system avoid utility review?

No automatic exemption applies. Parallel connected equipment may still need review, protection, studies and approval. Confirm current utility requirements and configuration before purchase or operation.

Can I put a BESS in an unused truck court or warehouse corner?

Only after site, code, fire, utility, lease and insurer review. Check access, trucks, drainage, exits, neighbors and electrical constraints. Vacant areas may serve circulation or have easement restrictions.

Are lithium ion storage systems free of fire risk?

No. Thermal runaway can release heat/gases, spread or reignite. Professional design, suitable equipment, code compliance, maintenance and emergency planning reduce but do not eliminate hazards.

Are UL 9540 and UL 9540A the same approval?

No. UL 9540 addresses system safety certification; UL 9540A tests thermal runaway fire propagation. Evidence must match the configuration and neither replaces permits nor establishes universal safety.

Does meeting NFPA 855 guarantee a permit?

No. Officials apply locally adopted codes, editions and amendments to each project. Utility, zoning, building and fire approvals are separate; additional documentation may be required.

Who receives savings when the tenant pays the electric bill?

The account and lease generally govern benefit allocation. Landlord spending does not automatically create savings or NOI. Agree on meter access, payments, baselines and responsibilities before installation.

Is leasing land for a grid battery the same as installing onsite storage?

No. Developers usually operate separate projects; landlords receive contractual rent, not automatic energy revenue or cheaper power. Review credit, options, easements, interconnection and removal obligations.

Will BESS automatically raise my building's value?

No. Buyers/lenders assess documented net benefits, condition, warranties, contracts and removal costs. Land restrictions, short support terms or unfavorable agreements can offset gains.

Does every commercial battery qualify for rebates or tax credits?

No. Law/program terms, equipment, ownership, timing and other conditions govern eligibility. Get utility/tax advice before purchase; model conditional incentives separately from committed funding.

Who pays to remove batteries when the project ends?

Contracts should assign removal, lawful handling, recycling, cleanup and restoration with financial security. Tenants, developers or manufacturers may lack funds when obligations arise.
