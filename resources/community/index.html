Buying and investing

Is a 10,000 to 20,000 SF building a sensible first industrial investment?

Buying and investing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

I own some residential rentals and want to move into industrial. Is a small single-tenant building too risky as a first deal?

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

From the user side: small buildings are where companies like mine live. When we looked, there were very few choices under 20,000 SF with a decent yard.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We see a lot of first-time industrial buyers at this size. The questions we ask are about the lease term versus the loan term, and your liquidity if the tenant leaves.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

It can be, if you underwrite the vacancy you will eventually have. A single-tenant building is either 100% leased or 0% leased, so the questions are how fast it would re-lease, what it would cost (downtime, improvements, commissions), and whether you could carry the debt and taxes meanwhile.

Small functional buildings in infill locations tend to have deep user demand, including owner-users who may buy it from you later. Read the single- versus multi-tenant discussion before you choose.

Two buildings are listed at the same cap rate. Why would one be the better buy?

Buying and investing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Same size, same county, same cap rate on the offering memorandum. How do I tell them apart?

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Look at what the tenants actually pay back. Two "NNN" leases can have very different recoveries once you read the CAM and roof language.

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Ask how happy the tenant is. If we are about to outgrow a space, nothing in the rent roll shows it.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

The cap rate is one number from one year. Compare the rent each tenant pays against market rent, the remaining lease term, renewal options, expense recoveries, the roof and pavement age, the tax situation after a sale, and the building’s functionality for the next tenant.

A building with rent below market and a short lease can be the better buy at the same cap rate, because the income has room to grow, provided you can afford the rollover. Use the cap-rate calculator on your own adjusted NOI, not the marketing number.

Should an investor ever buy a vacant industrial building?

Buying and investing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Vacant buildings seem cheaper per square foot. What is the catch?

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Financing is the catch. Without leases we lend on a lower value with more equity, or we need a bridge loan with a higher rate.

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

You are competing with people like me. We can pay more for a vacant building because we plan to use it.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Sometimes. It is a value-add or lease-up strategy, and the price has to pay for the risk: months of carry (taxes, insurance, utilities, debt), improvements, commissions, and free rent. It works best for functional buildings in locations with steady demand and when you have the capital to wait.

Expect to compete with owner-users, who often finance with SBA 504 loans and can pay more than an investor can justify on rent alone. See owner-user vs. investor buyers.

Should I buy industrial alone or with partners?

Buying and investing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

A few friends want to pool money. What should we work out before we buy?

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We will want to know who signs the guaranty and who controls decisions. Unclear governance slows loans down.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Partnerships work when decision rights are clear before money moves. Agree in writing on who manages, how major decisions are made (sale, refinancing, capital calls, leasing), how cash is distributed, what happens if a partner cannot fund a capital call, and how someone exits.

Raising money from passive investors can involve securities law, so talk to an attorney before you advertise a deal or accept investor funds.

How much cash should I keep in reserve after buying an industrial building?

Buying and investing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Is there a rule of thumb for reserves on a small industrial property?

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Roofs, dock levelers, overhead doors, and parking lots are the usual big-ticket items. Most surprises are one of those.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Many loans require replacement reserves or tenant-improvement and leasing reserves anyway, especially when lease terms are short.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

There is no universal number. Build your reserve from the property condition assessment’s capital schedule, your lease rollover (downtime, improvements, commissions for the next expiration), and several months of taxes and debt service in case a tenant leaves.

In Illinois, budget specifically for property tax increases after the sale. See the tax underwriting section.

Leasing and tenants

As a tenant, what lease terms matter more than the rent?

Leasing and tenants · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We are renewing a 25,000 SF lease. Rent gets all the attention internally. What else should we push on?

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Restoration at move-out. Tenants forget about it until they get the bill for removing racking anchors and offices.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

From the landlord side, flexibility costs money. Expansion and termination rights are worth asking for, but expect to pay for them.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Total occupancy cost and flexibility. Check the expense pass-throughs (taxes, insurance, CAM, management fees, capital items), the repair and replacement responsibilities for the roof, HVAC, and docks, any renewal and expansion rights, assignment and sublease rights, restoration obligations, and the rights to use outdoor storage and trailer parking.

Compare offers as an all-in cost over the full term. The lease cost section shows how.

My tenant is paying rent but has stopped operating in the building. Should I worry?

Leasing and tenants · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The lights are off and the trucks are gone, but rent arrives on time.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Walk the building. Vacant buildings get roof leaks, frozen pipes, and break-ins, and nobody reports them.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Check your loan documents. Some have occupancy or insurance conditions that a dark building can trigger.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Yes, it is a signal to plan, even though it is not a default. Read the lease for any continuous-operation requirement, insurance conditions on vacancy, maintenance obligations, and sublease rights. Then talk with the tenant: an early termination or a sublease to a new user may be better for both of you than waiting out the term.

Start marketing conversations early, because industrial space can take months to re-lease.

Our CAM reconciliation came in much higher than the estimate. What can we do?

Leasing and tenants · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The year-end true-up is several dollars per square foot higher than the budget.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Snow removal and property tax swings cause most big true-ups around Chicago. Ask for the detail by line item.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Ask for the reconciliation backup and compare it with the lease: which costs are recoverable, how your share is calculated, whether there are caps on controllable expenses, and whether capital replacements were passed through improperly. Many leases give tenants audit rights within a set time after receiving the statement.

In Chicagoland, property tax changes are often the biggest driver, so check whether the landlord appealed the assessment.

Can we sign a one-year lease on industrial space?

Leasing and tenants · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We need space for a project that may last 12 to 18 months.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Short terms are hard for landlords because the commission and improvement costs barely pay back. Expect as-is space and higher rent.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Look for space that is already built out, or for a sublease with a short remaining term.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Sometimes, but expect trade-offs: as-is space, higher rent, fewer concessions, and possibly a larger deposit. Subleases with 12 to 24 months remaining, older multi-tenant buildings, and third-party logistics (3PL) warehousing are common short-term options.

If the need may extend, negotiate a renewal option now.

Can we park trailers and store equipment outside a leased building?

Leasing and tenants · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Our operation needs space for about ten trailers and some equipment outside.

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We learned the hard way that the village cared even though the landlord did not.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Only if both the lease and the zoning allow it. Many municipalities restrict outdoor storage and trailer parking even in industrial districts, or they require screening, paving, and approval. Building and operations

Our roof is 20 years old. Repair it, restore it, or replace it?

Building and operations · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We have a few leaks on a 40,000 SF building and want to plan for the next 10 years.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Get a core cut and moisture survey before deciding. Wet insulation usually rules out a coating.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

If you might sell in a few years, a buyer will price the roof anyway. A transferable warranty helps.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Have a roofing consultant do an infrared or moisture survey and take core samples. If the insulation is mostly dry, a restoration or coating system may extend the roof’s life at lower cost; if it is wet, replacement is usually the better long-term answer.

Either way, document the work and keep the warranties, because buyers and lenders will ask for them. See physical due diligence.

Can we add dock doors to an existing industrial building?

Building and operations · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We have grade-level doors only and need to load semi-trailers.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Check the truck court first. A dock door is useless without room for the trailer to back in.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Often, yes. Common approaches are cutting the wall and excavating an exterior dock well, or building an interior pit with a leveler. Feasibility depends on the wall structure, the slab, drainage, utilities underground, truck-court depth, and permits.

Get an engineer and a contractor to scope it, and check stormwater rules for the dock well. Adding docks can widen a building’s tenant and buyer pool.

How long does an electrical service upgrade take for a warehouse?

Building and operations · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We need more power for equipment and EV forklift charging.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The utility timeline is usually the long pole, not the electrician.

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Switchgear and transformer lead times have been long in recent years. Order early.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Plan for months, not weeks. The timeline depends on whether the utility needs to upgrade its transformer or line, equipment lead times for switchgear and transformers, engineering and permits, and lease negotiations over who pays and who owns the upgrade.

If power is critical, confirm the available capacity with the utility before you sign a lease or purchase agreement.

Will our storage need a sprinkler upgrade in a building we are about to lease?

Building and operations · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We store plastic goods on racks about 24 feet high.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Prior tenants often stored something easier, so the existing system was fine for them. Ask for the sprinkler design data.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Possibly. Sprinkler adequacy depends on the commodity classification (plastics are typically more demanding), storage height and arrangement, building height, and water supply. Ask the landlord for the system’s design criteria and have a fire-protection engineer compare it with your storage plan before you sign.

Negotiate in the lease who pays if an upgrade or in-rack sprinklers are needed. The local fire department will have the final say.

We are moving out of our building. What improvements help it lease or sell?

We plan to lease or sell our building after we relocate.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Buyers notice deferred maintenance immediately: roof, docks, lighting, pavement.

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Clean, bright, and empty sells. LED lighting and working docks matter on a tour.

Illinois taxes and incentives

Our Cook County tax bill jumped after reassessment. Is an appeal worth it?

Illinois taxes and incentives · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Our assessed value rose much more than we expected.

Tax consultant perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Watch the township appeal window. In Cook it is short and the dates are published by the Assessor.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Appeals are routine for income property. Most owners appeal regularly.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Often it is worth reviewing. Compare the Assessor’s estimate of market value with what the property would actually sell for, given its income, vacancy, condition, and comparable sales. Appeals can be filed with the Assessor during the township window, then with the Board of Review, and potentially the Property Tax Appeal Board.

Many attorneys work on contingency. Details are in the appeals and deadlines section.

Can an owner-user get the Class 6b incentive when buying a vacant building?

Illinois taxes and incentives · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We are looking at a building in Cook County that has been empty for a while.

Tax consultant perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Timing is critical. The application has to be filed on time, and the municipality’s support has to be in place.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

It is possible. Class 6b can apply to qualifying industrial property that is reoccupied after a period of vacancy, as well as new construction and substantial rehabilitation, if the municipality supports it and the application is timely. The rules on vacancy length, special circumstances, and filing deadlines are specific.

Check the Cook County Assessor’s current incentive requirements and talk to the municipality early, before closing. See What is Class 6b?

How do I estimate taxes for a DuPage or Will County building I am buying?

Illinois taxes and incentives · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

I am used to Cook County. What changes in the collar counties?

Tax consultant perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The township assessor is your first call. The supervisor of assessments and the Board of Review come next.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Outside Cook, property is generally assessed at one-third of market value by township assessors, with county equalization. Estimate the market value an assessor might apply after your purchase, apply the assessment level and the equalization factor, subtract exemptions, and multiply by the current tax rate for the property’s tax code.

Then confirm the result with the township assessor’s office. The Cook vs. collar county section explains the differences.

Can a tenant appeal the property taxes on a building it leases?

Illinois taxes and incentives · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Tenant operations perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We pay the taxes under our NNN lease. The landlord does not seem interested in appealing.

Property manager perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Read the lease. Many leases say who controls appeals and how refunds are split.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Read the lease first: many NNN leases say who may file appeals and who receives refunds. Illinois appeal rules also determine who has standing. A tenant that pays the taxes often has a strong interest, and in practice many tenants ask the landlord to appeal or to allow an appeal at the tenant’s expense.

Talk to a property tax attorney about standing and deadlines before the appeal window closes.

Financing

How does an SBA 504 loan work for buying our own warehouse?

Financing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

We are a growing distributor and want to buy instead of lease.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The occupancy rules matter. For an existing building, the business generally needs to occupy at least 51%.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

An SBA 504 loan typically combines a bank first mortgage, a second loan from a Certified Development Company backed by the SBA, and a down payment from the business, often around 10% but higher in some cases. The 504 portion offers long-term fixed rates.

The business must meet SBA size and eligibility rules and generally occupy at least 51% of an existing building. See the SBA’s 504 program page and talk to a CDC early.

Recourse or non-recourse debt on a small industrial building?

Financing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

My bank offers a better rate with a full personal guaranty.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Smaller loans from banks are usually recourse. Non-recourse is more common on larger loans and with life companies or CMBS lenders.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Compare the whole package, not just the rate. Recourse debt from a local bank can come with flexibility, relationship value, and lower costs, but it puts your personal assets at risk. Non-recourse loans limit that risk but typically carry “bad boy” carve-outs, stricter terms, and prepayment penalties such as yield maintenance or defeasance.

Some lenders offer partial recourse or burn-offs once a property stabilizes.

How do prepayment penalties affect when I can sell?

Financing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

I want flexibility to sell in three to five years.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Step-down penalties, such as 3-2-1, are common on bank loans. Fixed-rate CMBS and life company loans tend to use yield maintenance or defeasance.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

A large prepayment penalty can effectively lock you into a hold or require a buyer to assume your loan. Match the loan’s prepayment terms to your realistic hold period, and ask whether the loan can be assumed by a buyer.

Model the penalty in your exit analysis at different interest rates, because yield maintenance gets more expensive when rates fall.

Why do lenders discount income from leases that expire before the loan?

Financing · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

A frequent borrower question we answer.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

This surprised me on my first deal. The building was full but the loan came in lower than expected.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Because the lender is underwriting the risk that the income stops. If the leases roll during the loan term, lenders may size the loan on lower rent or vacancy, require leasing reserves, add a cash sweep, or shorten the term.

Borrowers can improve proceeds by renewing leases early, showing tenant commitment, or providing reserves. See how lenders size loans.

Another common borrower question.

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Ours found an old underground tank. It delayed closing by a few weeks while it was investigated.

Selling and exit

We own our building and are thinking about retiring. Sell, sale-leaseback, or lease it out?

Selling and exit · How each side may see it: simulated editorial perspectives

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

The company may be sold separately from the real estate.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

A buyer of your company might want a lease from you. That lease can be what makes the real estate valuable.

Tax consultant perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Talk to your accountant about the tax basis and depreciation recapture before deciding.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

Each path has trade-offs. A sale to an owner-user can pay well if the building is functional and you vacate. A sale-leaseback sets a lease and sells to an investor, which can work alongside a company sale. Keeping the building and leasing it gives income but keeps you as a landlord.

Coordinate the real estate plan with the business sale and your tax advisor. See sale-leasebacks and 1031 exchanges.

Private investor perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

I am worried a short lease hurts the price.

Commercial lender perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

A buyer’s lender will discount that income too, which affects what the buyer can pay.

Owner-user perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Some buyers like me want the building when the lease ends.

Editorial note by Pavlo RospopaSite author guidance, not an accepted member answer

A renewal at market rent usually maximizes investor pricing, because it removes the biggest risk. But the short lease can attract owner-users who plan to occupy the building after expiration. Price both options: investor value with a renewal versus owner-user value with a planned vacancy.

I am selling and doing a 1031 exchange. How do I avoid running out of time?

The 45-day identification window worries me.

Tax consultant perspectiveSimulated viewpoint, not a real member or quote

Set up your qualified intermediary before closing on the sale. You cannot touch the proceeds.
