Use this regional hub to investigate warehouse, distribution, manufacturing, and flex property in Cook, DeKalb, DuPage, Grundy, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties. Start with the parcel and the operation, then identify the authority and records that actually apply.

Establish jurisdiction before comparing locations

Record the street address, every property index number (PIN), county, and incorporated municipality or unincorporated status. A postal city, listing label, or county tax record does not establish the zoning authority. Municipalities can cross county lines, and a project can involve several parcels. Check each parcel rather than assigning the whole site from one address.

For property inside a city or village, start with its planning, zoning, and building staff. For unincorporated property, start with the county department linked below. Confirm any shared-service arrangement and separate fire, health, highway, utility, or stormwater approvals. County assessment and mapping resources can be useful even where municipal zoning applies.

Use GIS as a research aid, not a survey, title opinion, zoning approval, or guarantee of utility service. A comprehensive plan describes policy or future land use; it is not the same as today's zoning or permission to operate. Municipal examples below are orientation points, not recommendations or assurances that industrial space is available.

County assessment offices

Cook County has an elected county assessor. In the other eight counties, township assessors typically set initial values and the county supervisor of assessments or chief county assessment officer supervises, equalizes, and publishes them. Start with the county office below to identify parcels, assessment history, exemptions, and appeal deadlines; verify tax bills with the county treasurer separately.

Cook County: distinguish Chicago from suburban authority

Chicago and suburban locations such as Bedford Park require separate local research. Chicago's zoning map and Department of Buildings are city resources, not countywide permitting systems. Cook County Building and Zoning directs incorporated-property inquiries to the applicable municipality and handles unincorporated areas, with a separate role for Forest Preserve property.

For a city distribution or service operation, trace the final approach from the public road to the loading area: curb access, backing movements, delivery windows, pedestrian conflicts, and any street-use requirements. For a suburban yard or warehouse, ask about outdoor storage and trailer parking independently of indoor warehouse permission. Neither a Chicago mailing address nor an industrial tax classification resolves those questions.

  • Cook County Building and Zoning: county jurisdiction and building/zoning services.
  • Cook County Assessor: identify parcels and assessment records; verify tax bills separately.
  • Chicago zoning map and Chicago Department of Buildings: city-specific starting points.

DeKalb County: separate mapped land use from approvals

DeKalb and Sycamore provide municipal starting points; sites outside municipal limits require county confirmation. DeKalb County Community Development reviews construction in unincorporated areas and administers county planning, zoning, subdivision, and stormwater regulations.

The county's Property Data and Maps page separates parcel information from Community Development mapping. Use those resources together to identify the site and inspect zoning and development layers. A mapped enterprise zone or a future-use designation does not by itself establish incentive eligibility or an approved industrial operation.

For a new manufacturing site or warehouse expansion, define the construction footprint, process-water needs, electrical loads, drainage changes, and required access before an approval discussion. Ask the responsible authority and utilities what can be served at this specific parcel; nearby development is not evidence that capacity is reserved for the project.

  • DeKalb County Assessment Office: parcel identification, assessment records, and appeal information.
  • DeKalb County Community Development: planning, building, and land-use responsibilities.
  • DeKalb County Property Data and Maps: parcel, tax, land-record, and Community Development map entry points.

DuPage County: reconcile municipal limits and building fit

Addison and Carol Stream are examples of DuPage County municipalities to research through their own departments. DuPage County Building and Zoning provides building permitting for unincorporated areas. Its Interactive Map Gallery links a parcel viewer for locating and checking the parcels under review.

For an existing warehouse or office/industrial flex building, compare the proposed office, storage, and production mix with the approved occupancy and required parking. Document whether a conversion changes fire separation, accessibility, ventilation, or loading needs. A flexible floor plan does not necessarily mean every activity is allowed.

Plot the actual lot, driveway, truck court, and easements before assuming there is room for more docks or trailer storage. When comparing a DuPage site with a neighboring-county option, keep the parcel and permitting records separate even if both share a familiar municipal or corridor label.

  • DuPage County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal procedures.
  • DuPage County Building and Zoning: building, zoning, and planning services.
  • DuPage County Interactive Map Gallery: county maps and the linked Parcel Viewer.

Grundy County: investigate site development and approval paths

Morris is a municipal starting point within Grundy County. For a county-administered site, the Land Use Department separates Building, Planning and Zoning, and Environmental and Resource Conservation functions. Contact the right office for the proposed work rather than treating a zoning inquiry as a complete development review.

The Planning and Zoning Office describes hearings for map amendments, subdivision plats, special uses, and variances. Its page distinguishes recommendations to committees and the County Board from variance decisions by the Zoning Board of Appeals. If an industrial project needs relief or rezoning, confirm the applicable sequence before committing to a possession or construction date.

For a distribution yard or undeveloped manufacturing site, include pavement, grading, drainage, outdoor storage, and emergency access in the concept plan. Review flood mapping and site elevations with qualified professionals; neither vacant land nor an existing paved yard establishes that expansion is permissible.

  • Grundy County Supervisor of Assessments: online assessment inquiry, forms, and Board of Review information.
  • Grundy County Land Use Department: department structure and building-related starting points.
  • Grundy County Planning and Zoning Office: hearing types and decision paths.

Kane County: resolve multi-county names and construction scope

Geneva is a Kane County orientation point. Aurora and Elgin searches need particular care: both municipalities extend beyond Kane County. Find the actual county and municipal limits for the parcel before ordering records or estimating taxes. Kane's map directory supports municipality, address, and parcel research.

Kane County Building and Zoning provides property-research and permit instructions for unincorporated sites, including new construction, remodeling, repair, and demolition. Confirm whether a proposed equipment installation, mezzanine, dock alteration, or production conversion requires review; a prior tenant's permit is not approval for your project.

For operations whose routes cross the Fox River, test the specific crossing and local approach with the intended vehicle rather than relying on distance between towns. Check road authority, posted restrictions, and documented access rights. A mapped route does not establish legal truck access or a dependable delivery time.

  • Kane County Supervisor of Assessments: parcel and assessment research for Kane County portions of a property.
  • Kane County Building and Zoning Division: unincorporated property research, permit instructions, and references.
  • Kane County maps: official entry point for parcel and municipality mapping.

Kendall County: verify service authority and expansion assumptions

Yorkville and Oswego are useful municipal starting points for Kendall County searches. Check the specific parcel when a search reaches a county boundary; a municipal name is not a substitute for county identification. The county GIS department provides mapping resources for that investigation.

Kendall's Planning, Building and Zoning Department describes services for unincorporated areas and the incorporated villages of Millbrook and Plattville. This is a reason to confirm service arrangements rather than assuming that incorporation always rules out a county role. Ask which office handles each zoning, building, and inspection step for the address.

When evaluating land for a warehouse or a manufacturing expansion, request written utility availability, extension responsibilities, and the approvals for subdivision or site changes. Review the county's Land Resource Management Plan as planning context, then confirm current zoning and any required relief. Budget approvals and infrastructure separately from the advertised land price.

  • Kendall County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, township assessor contacts, and appeals.
  • Kendall County Planning, Building and Zoning: service area, land-use planning, and department guidance.
  • Kendall County Geographic Information Systems: official mapping and property-information entry point.

Lake County, Illinois: use early assistance for complex projects

Waukegan and Gurnee are Lake County, Illinois municipal examples. This section does not cover Indiana's Lake County or Wisconsin property. Lake County's permitting page directs incorporated-site inquiries to the village or city and describes county permits for unincorporated sites.

For a complicated nonresidential project in unincorporated Lake County, the county describes Early Assistance meetings coordinated across applicable county departments. Bring a specific site and an operational concept: a manufacturing process, a warehouse change of use, or a flex conversion needs more detail than a square-footage target.

The permitting page distinguishes change-of-use, site-development, building, conditional-use, and other approvals. Ask which apply to production, earthwork, outdoor activity, or a new storage arrangement. Use the county's mapping hub for initial geographic research, and verify drainage, wetland, and flood questions with the appropriate authority and specialists.

  • Lake County, Illinois Chief County Assessment Office: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal procedures.
  • Lake County Permits, Approvals and Early Assistance: unincorporated permitting and coordinated project guidance.
  • Lake County, Illinois mapping and open data: official geographic-data starting point.

McHenry County: combine land-use maps with permit history

Woodstock and Crystal Lake are municipal starting points within McHenry County. The county Planning and Development Department directs incorporated-site inquiries to the city or village; its permitting services apply to unincorporated areas.

The county GIS Interactive Maps directory distinguishes Athena property information from PlanDev zoning and land-use research. Read current zoning separately from proposed land use. For an existing industrial building, use the department's Electronic Records Search to investigate available closed permits and zoning petitions, then request missing records and confirmation of present approvals.

For distribution or storage uses, review the full route between the site and destinations, not just a regional highway label. Include local truck access, drainage, loading-hour constraints, and neighboring uses in the review. For a manufacturing conversion, reconcile the record history with the proposed equipment, wastewater, ventilation, and fire-protection needs.

  • McHenry County Office of Assessments: assessment notices, parcel inquiry, and complaint deadlines.
  • McHenry County Planning and Development: county permitting and land-use services.
  • McHenry County GIS Interactive Maps: property and planning map directory.
  • McHenry County Electronic Records Search: available permit and zoning records, not proof of current building condition.

Will County: separate freight proximity from usable access

Romeoville is a Will County municipal example. Joliet searches can cross into Kendall County, so verify each parcel rather than assigning the entire city to Will. For incorporated sites, begin with the municipality; use county land-use staff for county-administered zoning questions.

Will County's Current Planning and Zoning section describes map amendments, special uses, variances, and building-permit zoning review. It maintains official zoning maps and records. Request the zoning district, relevant approvals, and any conditions affecting yards, parking, setbacks, or proposed activity; an industrial district label alone is not a project approval.

For a warehouse or distribution operation advertised near freight infrastructure, confirm the actual gate-to-destination route, terminal access arrangements where relevant, staging, queuing, and legal trailer parking. Nearby rail facilities do not mean a building has rail service or access rights. For manufacturing, investigate utility and process approvals independently of freight proximity.

  • Will County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal information.
  • Will County Land Use Department: official department entry point.
  • Will County Current Planning and Zoning: zoning applications, permit review, plans, and records.

Match the operation in every county

Warehouse and storage
Identify commodities, rack layout, storage height, slab loads, and fire-protection design. Verify usable clear height below obstructions and whether approved storage arrangements fit the inventory.
Distribution and logistics
Model peak vehicle movements, dock appointments, truck turning, queuing, trailer storage, and employee access. Test routes at relevant times; neither county location nor highway proximity guarantees travel time.
Manufacturing
Give reviewers equipment and process details, electrical demand, water use, discharge, emissions, noise, vibration, and operating hours. Confirm utility capacity and applicable permits before assuming an industrial building can accommodate production.
Flex space
Define the office/industrial mix, customer visits, parking, occupancy classification, ventilation, and separation between uses. Existing office improvements do not establish permission for assembly, production, or storage.

Use the industrial building-fit checklist for measurements, systems, loading, and inspection questions. County research complements that checklist; it does not replace an engineer's, environmental professional's, or code specialist's review.

Build a property-specific diligence file

  1. Identity and authority: retain the PINs, boundary evidence, zoning designation, responsible offices, and written responses about the exact use.
  2. Records and site risk: collect approvals, permit history, title and survey information, access rights, drainage documents, and tax records for every parcel. Use the correct county assessor and treasurer, not Cook County by default.
  3. Flood and environmental review: start with the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and EPA All Appropriate Inquiries guidance, then commission appropriate site-specific work. Maps and an assessment are not guarantees that all risk is absent.
  4. Cost and schedule: itemize improvements, utility upgrades, permit dependencies, and responsibility for ongoing maintenance. Use the leasing guide or purchase-diligence guide for transaction-specific questions.

Record the source, access date, parcel searched, verified finding, and unresolved question. Official pages and applications can change; if a map or portal is unavailable, contact the named county department. For the broader research process, see commercial property research.
