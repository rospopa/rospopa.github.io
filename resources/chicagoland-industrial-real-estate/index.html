Chicagoland industrial real estate: nine Illinois counties

Educational research, not listings or current market statistics.

By Pavlo Rospopa · Last updated October 10, 2026

In brief

Research warehouse, distribution, manufacturing, and flex property in Cook, DeKalb, DuPage, Grundy, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry, and Will counties. Start with the parcel and operation, then identify the relevant authorities and records.

  • Covers nine Illinois counties: Cook, DeKalb, DuPage, Grundy, Kane, Kendall, Lake, McHenry, and Will.
  • Confirm county, municipality or unincorporated status, and every PIN. Authority follows the parcel.
  • County sections link official planning, zoning, parcel, and assessment offices.
  • Aurora, Elgin, and Joliet cross county lines. Lake County here means Illinois, not Indiana.
  • Owner lens: A sale or expansion can involve separate municipal, county, fire, utility, stormwater, and tax reviews. Confirm parcel boundaries, service providers, and responsibility for each approval before committing capital.

Establish jurisdiction before comparing locations

Record address, every property index number (PIN), county and incorporation status. Postal cities, listing labels and tax records do not establish zoning authority. Check every parcel; municipalities may cross counties and projects may span parcels.

Start with municipal planning, zoning and building staff for incorporated sites, or the county department below for unincorporated sites. Confirm shared services and separate fire, health, highway, utility and stormwater approvals. County assessment and maps can also help under municipal zoning.

GIS aids research; it is not a survey, title opinion, zoning approval or utility guarantee. Comprehensive plans state policy or future use, not current zoning or operating permission. Municipal examples orient research, not recommendations or available-space claims.

County assessment offices

Cook has an elected assessor. In the other eight counties, township assessors typically set initial values; county supervisors of assessments or chief assessment officers supervise, equalize and publish them. County offices below provide parcels, assessment history, exemptions and appeal deadlines. Verify bills with the treasurer.

Cook County: distinguish Chicago from suburban authority

Chicago and suburbs such as Bedford Park require separate research. Chicago's zoning map and Department of Buildings are city resources, not countywide permitting systems. Cook County Building and Zoning directs incorporated-property inquiries to the municipality and handles unincorporated areas, with a separate role for Forest Preserve property.

For city distribution or service operations, trace the public-road approach to loading, including curb access, backing, delivery windows, pedestrian conflicts, and street-use rules. For suburban sites, verify outdoor storage and trailer parking separately from indoor warehouse permission. A Chicago mailing address or industrial tax classification settles neither question.

  • Cook County Building and Zoning: county jurisdiction and building/zoning services.
  • Cook County Assessor: identify parcels and assessment records; verify tax bills separately.
  • Chicago zoning map and Chicago Department of Buildings: city-specific starting points.

DeKalb County: separate mapped land use from approvals

DeKalb and Sycamore are municipal starting points; confirm sites outside municipal limits with the county. DeKalb County Community Development reviews unincorporated construction and administers county planning, zoning, subdivision, and stormwater rules.

Use the county's Property Data and Maps and Community Development maps together to identify parcels and review zoning and development layers. An enterprise-zone map or future-use designation does not establish incentive eligibility or approval for an industrial use.

For new manufacturing or warehouse expansion, define footprint, process-water needs, electrical loads, drainage changes and access. Confirm parcel service with authorities and utilities; nearby development does not reserve capacity.

  • DeKalb County Assessment Office: parcel identification, assessment records, and appeal information.
  • DeKalb County Community Development: planning, building, and land-use responsibilities.
  • DeKalb County Property Data and Maps: parcel, tax, land-record, and Community Development map entry points.

DuPage County: reconcile municipal limits and building fit

Research Addison and Carol Stream through their municipal departments. DuPage County Building and Zoning permits unincorporated areas; its Interactive Map Gallery links a parcel viewer.

For warehouse or office/industrial flex, compare the proposed office, storage, and production mix with approved occupancy and parking. Check whether conversion affects fire separation, accessibility, ventilation, or loading. A flexible plan does not make every activity permissible.

Plot the lot, driveway, truck court, and easements before assuming room for docks or trailer storage. Keep parcel and permit records separate across counties, even when corridor labels match.

  • DuPage County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal procedures.
  • DuPage County Building and Zoning: building, zoning, and planning services.
  • DuPage County Interactive Map Gallery: county maps and the linked Parcel Viewer.

Grundy County: investigate site development and approval paths

Morris is a municipal starting point. For county-administered sites, Grundy County's Land Use Department separates Building, Planning and Zoning, and Environmental and Resource Conservation. Contact the office responsible for the work; a zoning inquiry is not full development review.

Planning and Zoning holds hearings for map amendments, subdivision plats, special uses and variances. Its page distinguishes committee and County Board recommendations from Zoning Board of Appeals variance decisions. Confirm relief or rezoning sequences before committing possession or construction dates.

For a distribution yard or undeveloped manufacturing site, plan for pavement, grading, drainage, outdoor storage, and emergency access. Have qualified professionals review flood maps and elevations. Vacant land or existing pavement does not establish expansion rights.

  • Grundy County Supervisor of Assessments: online assessment inquiry, forms, and Board of Review information.
  • Grundy County Land Use Department: department structure and building-related starting points.
  • Grundy County Planning and Zoning Office: hearing types and decision paths.

Kane County: resolve multi-county names and construction scope

Geneva is a Kane County reference point. Aurora and Elgin cross county lines; identify the parcel's county and municipal limits before ordering records or estimating taxes. Kane's map directory supports municipality, address, and parcel research.

Kane County Building and Zoning provides unincorporated-site research and permit instructions for construction, remodeling, repair, and demolition. Check whether equipment installation, mezzanines, dock changes, or production conversion need review. A prior tenant's permit does not approve your project.

Where routes cross the Fox River, test the specific crossing and approach with the intended vehicle. Check road authority, restrictions, and access rights; a mapped route does not establish legal truck access or delivery time.

  • Kane County Supervisor of Assessments: parcel and assessment research for Kane County portions of a property.
  • Kane County Building and Zoning Division: unincorporated property research, permit instructions, and references.
  • Kane County maps: official entry point for parcel and municipality mapping.

Kendall County: verify service authority and expansion assumptions

Yorkville and Oswego are municipal starting points. At county boundaries, verify the parcel through Kendall County GIS rather than relying on a municipal name.

Kendall's Planning, Building and Zoning Department serves unincorporated areas and the incorporated villages of Millbrook and Plattville. Confirm which office handles zoning, building, and inspection for the address.

For warehouse land or manufacturing expansion, request written utility availability, extension responsibilities, and subdivision or site-change approvals. Use the Land Resource Management Plan as context, then confirm current zoning and required relief. Budget approvals and infrastructure separately from land price.

  • Kendall County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, township assessor contacts, and appeals.
  • Kendall County Planning, Building and Zoning: service area, land-use planning, and department guidance.
  • Kendall County Geographic Information Systems: official mapping and property-information entry point.

Lake County, Illinois: use early assistance for complex projects

Waukegan and Gurnee are Lake County, Illinois examples, not Indiana's Lake County or Wisconsin. The county directs incorporated-site inquiries to cities or villages and describes permits for unincorporated sites.

Early Assistance meetings coordinate departments for complex unincorporated Lake County nonresidential projects. Bring a site and operational concept; manufacturing, warehouse use changes and flex conversion need more than square footage.

The permitting page distinguishes change-of-use, site-development, building, conditional-use, and other approvals. Ask which apply to production, earthwork, outdoor activity, or new storage. Use the county map for geographic research and confirm drainage, wetlands, and flood issues with authorities and specialists.

  • Lake County, Illinois Chief County Assessment Office: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal procedures.
  • Lake County Permits, Approvals and Early Assistance: unincorporated permitting and coordinated project guidance.
  • Lake County, Illinois mapping and open data: official geographic-data starting point.

McHenry County: combine land-use maps with permit history

Woodstock and Crystal Lake are municipal starting points. McHenry County Planning and Development directs incorporated-site inquiries to the city or village and handles unincorporated permitting.

McHenry GIS separates Athena property information from PlanDev zoning and land-use research. Check current zoning separately from proposed land use. Use Electronic Records Search for available closed permits and zoning petitions; request missing records and confirmation of current approvals.

For distribution or storage, review the full route, local truck access, drainage, loading-hour limits, and neighboring uses. For manufacturing conversion, compare records with equipment, wastewater, ventilation, and fire-protection needs.

  • McHenry County Office of Assessments: assessment notices, parcel inquiry, and complaint deadlines.
  • McHenry County Planning and Development: county permitting and land-use services.
  • McHenry County GIS Interactive Maps: property and planning map directory.
  • McHenry County Electronic Records Search: available permit and zoning records, not proof of current building condition.

Will County: separate freight proximity from usable access

Romeoville is a Will County reference point. Joliet crosses into Kendall County, so verify each parcel. For incorporated sites, start with the municipality; ask county land-use staff about county-administered zoning.

Will County Current Planning and Zoning handles map amendments, special uses, variances, and building-permit zoning review, and maintains zoning maps and records. Request the district, approvals, and conditions for yards, parking, setbacks, and proposed uses; an industrial label is not project approval.

For sites marketed near freight infrastructure, verify gate-to-destination routes, terminal access, staging, queuing, and legal trailer parking. Nearby rail does not establish service or access rights. Review manufacturing utilities and process approvals separately.

  • Will County Supervisor of Assessments: assessment records, exemptions, and appeal information.
  • Will County Land Use Department: official department entry point.
  • Will County Current Planning and Zoning: zoning applications, permit review, plans, and records.

Match the operation in every county

Warehouse and storage
Identify commodities, rack layout, storage height, slab loads, and fire-protection design. Verify usable clear height below obstructions and whether approved storage arrangements fit the inventory.
Distribution and logistics
Model peak vehicle movements, dock appointments, truck turning, queuing, trailer storage, and employee access. Test routes at relevant times; neither county location nor highway proximity guarantees travel time.
Manufacturing
Give reviewers equipment and process details, electrical demand, water use, discharge, emissions, noise, vibration, and operating hours. Confirm utility capacity and applicable permits before assuming an industrial building can accommodate production.
Flex space
Define the office/industrial mix, customer visits, parking, occupancy classification, ventilation, and separation between uses. Existing office improvements do not establish permission for assembly, production, or storage.

Use the industrial building-fit checklist for measurements, systems, loading and inspections. County research complements it, not engineering, environmental or code specialist review.

Build a property-specific diligence file

  1. Identity and authority: retain the PINs, boundary evidence, zoning designation, responsible offices, and written responses about the exact use.
  2. Records and site risk: collect approvals, permit history, title and survey information, access rights, drainage documents, and tax records for every parcel. Use the correct county assessor and treasurer, not Cook County by default.
  3. Flood and environmental review: start with the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and EPA All Appropriate Inquiries guidance, then commission appropriate site-specific work. Maps and an assessment are not guarantees that all risk is absent.
  4. Cost and schedule: itemize improvements, utility upgrades, permit dependencies, and responsibility for ongoing maintenance. Use the leasing guide or purchase-diligence guide for transaction-specific questions.

Record the source, access date, parcel searched, verified finding, and unresolved question. Official pages and applications can change; if a map or portal is unavailable, contact the named county department. For the broader research process, see commercial property research.
