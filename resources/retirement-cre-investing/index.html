Owner education: retirement and industrial investing

Retirement planning with commercial real estate

Commercial real estate can support retirement, but rent is not guaranteed. Existing property and IRA or employer plan investments have different ownership, tax and distribution rules.

Updated October 8, 2026. General U.S. education with Illinois owner considerations, not personalized retirement, securities, tax, legal or investment advice.

Planning the building side of retirement

In brief

  • Compare retirement spending with cash after operating costs, debt, capital work, leasing and taxes, not gross rent or NOI alone.
  • A self directed IRA is an investment arrangement within an IRA, not a separate exemption from prohibited transaction or distribution rules.
  • Illiquid buildings, loans and private funds can complicate required minimum distributions and emergency spending.
  • A 1031 exchange is not a retirement account rollover and does not eliminate investment risk.
  • Owner lens: Arrange management for retirement, illness or death. A single industrial tenant leaving can interrupt income when your household needs it.

Is your retirement plan based on property income or a retirement account?

Personal or taxable entity property can fund retirement without becoming an IRA. Its owner receives rent and sale proceeds and may hire a manager, refinance or sell, subject to leases, loans, entity agreements and taxes.

An IRA or qualified plan receives its investment's rent and sale proceeds; household access requires an account distribution. Keep property underwriting, account compliance and household withdrawals separate.

Separate owner occupied business earnings from rent. A business sale need not include the building, but retaining it can make retirement income depend on the buyer as tenant. See investment diligence and the lease checklist.

Which retirement plans can hold commercial real estate?

Eligibility depends on tax law, plan documents and provider capabilities. Many brokerage accounts offer REITs or real estate funds, not direct buildings. Confirm your IRA or workplace plan accepts deeds, private partnerships or loans.

Traditional versus Roth concerns tax timing, not property performance. Compare current taxes, future distributions, conversion costs, fees and cash with a CPA. This guide prescribes no account or contribution amount; check current year limits and income thresholds.

Can rental income or a building sale fund retirement plan contributions?

Ordinary rent generally is not IRA compensation or self employed plan earned income. Sale proceeds, appreciation, cash and real estate professional status alone do not establish qualifying compensation.

Eligible wages or self employment earnings, including some active real estate business income, may support contributions; sufficient joint filer compensation may permit spousal IRAs. Have a CPA classify activity, payroll and entity income, not relabel rent or invent fees. For eligibility, deductions and limits, see IRS Publication 590-A and Publication 560.

Contributions, transfers, rollovers and conversions are different

Transfers and rollovers move eligible retirement assets, unlike annual contributions. Check distribution permission, receiving account acceptance, withholding and deadlines; coordinate providers directly to avoid personal receipt. IRA rollover frequency rules vary by transfer type; RMDs cannot be rolled over.

Roth conversions can create taxable income; illiquid property needs supported valuation and tax cash. You cannot sell personal property to your IRA or contribute it ordinarily. Seek advice before contracts or title transfers.

What personal use and related party transactions are prohibited?

IRA property is not for personal or business use. Prohibited transactions cover improper use and disqualified persons: owners, certain fiduciaries, spouses, ancestors, lineal descendants and their spouses, and certain controlled entities. Relatives' status varies; indirect personal benefit can still violate rules.

  • Do not sell your building to your IRA, buy its building personally, or assume leasing to your business or a disqualified person is permitted.
  • Do not personally guarantee account borrowing, support it with personal credit, or pledge account assets for personal debt.
  • Do not use account property personally, take its rent for household use, or casually pay its bills personally.
  • Do not assume you can perform repairs, pay yourself a management fee or provide services through your company. Administrative oversight and furnishing services require fact specific specialist review.
  • Review co investment, partnerships, family ownership, fees and IRA owned LLC arrangements before commitments. An LLC does not remove prohibited transaction rules.

Market rent or fairness cannot cure prohibited transactions. An owner or beneficiary violation can terminate IRA status from the year's first day, distributing assets at fair market value under IRS guidance. Income and early distribution taxes may follow; qualified plan consequences differ but remain serious.

Before purchase, confirm buyer and title names, signers, payment paths, reserves and custodian procedures for leases, vendors, loans and closing. Acceptance is neither a legal opinion nor an investment endorsement.

Can real estate inside an IRA owe tax before a distribution?

Yes. Ordinary real property rent generally is excluded from unrelated business taxable income, but debt, operating businesses, certain service intensive activities or partnerships can change treatment. UBIT and unrelated debt financed income (UDFI) can affect traditional and Roth IRAs.

Account loans generally must avoid personal guarantees and prohibited credit, often with only account collateral recourse. Leverage is not automatically tax free. Calculate debt financed rent and gains using acquisition indebtedness and adjusted basis, not cap rates or original loan percentages.

IRS Publication 598 covers exclusions and exceptions. Generally, $1,000 or more of unrelated trade/business gross income triggers Form 990-T filing, not just net profit above $1,000. Coordinate filing, estimated payments, allocation and funding with the CPA and custodian.

Certain qualified retirement trusts can meet the Section 514(c)(9) real property debt exception; IRAs cannot. Solo 401(k) investments, particularly partnerships or operating activities, are not automatically UBIT exempt.

IRA depreciation and losses generally are not personal deductions. Review account deductions separately; retained traditional IRA income and later distributions differ. Qualified Roth distributions require age and holding conditions, without curing prohibited transactions.

How much commercial property cash is actually available for retirement?

Budget household spending, health care, taxes and irregular expenses, less dependable other income. Test property cash against this gap in ordinary and adverse cases. A cap rate measures income relative to value, not a safe withdrawal rate.

Subtract debt service, capital funding and leasing costs from documented NOI, without double counting reserves and funded projects. Estimate taxes and withdrawals separately. LLC distributions can include refinancing cash or returned capital, not just profit.

Direct buildings, REITs or private real estate: what changes in retirement?

Compare control, management, liquidity and concentration. One building concentrates tenant, local market and condition exposure; diversified funds can reduce that concentration without eliminating real estate, leverage or market risk.

How do RMDs and valuations work when a retirement account owns property?

Illiquidity does not excuse RMDs from traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs and many employer plans. Birth year and law set starting age, not retirement: generally 73 for 1951 through 1959 births, 75 for 1960 or later, earlier for earlier cohorts.

Roth IRAs and designated Roth plan accounts have no original owner lifetime RMDs under current rules, but beneficiaries face distribution requirements. Workplace plans may permit employed owners to delay, subject to plan terms and the 5% owner exception; traditional IRAs generally do not. Confirm dates with your administrator and IRS guidance.

Value the asset and identify the source of the distribution

RMDs generally use the prior December 31 account value and IRS distribution factor. Arrange supportable valuations and custodian reporting for deeds, private interests and property loans before reporting and distribution deadlines; do not assume unchanged purchase prices.

Rent or liquid assets can fund withdrawals. Generally, calculate each traditional IRA's RMD before aggregating your own accounts. Extra IRA withdrawals cannot satisfy a 401(k) RMD; inherited accounts have additional restrictions. Household cash outside the account does not satisfy distributions.

Custodians may permit properly valued in kind distributions after title, valuation and tax review, without providing tax cash. Shared account and personal ownership adds prohibited transaction risk. Do not simply deed yourself the building.

Avoid deadline driven sales and tax surprises

The first RMD generally may wait until the following April 1, but the next is due December 31 that year, potentially combining two in one tax year. Missed RMDs can trigger excise tax; promptly review correction and relief. Late tenant distributions or sales do not extend deadlines.

Coordinate withdrawals, loan maturities and lease expirations. Before age 59 1/2, withdrawals may incur income and early distribution taxes unless an exception applies; account types differ. Maintain accessible emergency cash.

Should an owner hold, sell or exchange industrial property before retirement?

Compare after tax cash, risk, workload and family needs, not just sale price. Record liquidity dates, vacancy management, capital work and loan maturities near retirement.

Keep the building with professional management

Arrange management, delegated authority, vendor controls, emergency coverage and reporting. Review expirations, renewal options, guarantees and landlord duties. Holding preserves potential income but concentrates capital and retains leasing, financing and condition risk. NNN still requires oversight.

Sell and redeploy cash outside the property

Estimate payoff, prepayment charges, brokerage/closing costs, basis, depreciation related tax, and federal/state taxes to calculate retirement proceeds. Gain is not proceeds minus the loan. Compare reinvestment and withdrawals with an adviser without assuming future returns.

Consider a qualifying 1031 exchange, not an IRA rollover

A 1031 exchange can defer eligible gain on qualifying business/investment real property. Typically, identify replacement within 45 days and receive it by the earlier of 180 days or the return due date, including extensions. Arrange a qualified intermediary and tax review before closing; receiving or controlling cash can defeat the exchange.

Deferral is not forgiveness; reinvestment reduces available retirement cash. Cash or net debt relief can create gain under the full calculation. Review related parties, entity ownership and replacement eligibility. Deadlines should not force weak purchases; DSTs, REITs and funds do not automatically qualify.

1031 does not move taxable property into an IRA. Distributions, rollovers and contributions follow separate rules. Account sales retain proceeds inside, potentially subject to account tax; taxable owner exchange strategies do not automatically apply.

Coordinate the building with a business sale

Compare selling business and building, retaining the building under lease, or a sale and leaseback. Leasebacks impose business rent, not free retirement income. Counsel must review buyer credit, rent, term, maintenance, guarantees, assignment and later business sales. Separate taxable planning from IRA related party restrictions.

Can a lazy 1031 reduce landlord work in retirement?

Lazy 1031 informally means qualifying exchanges with less daily management, not an IRS program. Options include eligible DSTs, qualifying ownership arrangements or professionally managed direct property. Net leases shift costs, not tenant, repair, financing or resale risks.

DST eligibility requires specific conditions. Tradeoffs include less control, sponsor fees, debt and restricted transfers. Distributions may include returned capital or borrowing and are not guaranteed. Operational passivity does not ensure liquidity, safety or passive tax classification.

Compare taxable sale cash, exchanges and continued ownership before deadlines. Ordinary REIT shares and partnership interests are not replacement real property; Section 721/UPREIT and IRA rollovers are separate. See lazy 1031 and DSTs for deadlines, boot, basis, loss carryforwards and adviser questions.

Will depreciation losses reduce my retirement income taxes?

Paper deductions may not offset pension, IRA or conversion income. Rentals generally are passive unless excepted; portfolio interest/dividends are not passive income. The active participation allowance is generally up to $25,000, subject to MAGI phaseouts and other requirements. Real estate professional status and material participation require separate annual tests; retirement confers neither.

Nonresidential GDS buildings generally use 39 year straight line, mid month depreciation, excluding land. Supported cost segregation may accelerate eligible shorter life assets. Eligible property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025 can qualify for restored 100% bonus under detailed rules, not expensing an ordinary entire warehouse. Illinois modifications can differ.

Allowed or allowable depreciation reduces basis. Qualifying recapture can be ordinary income; unrecaptured Section 1250 gain carries a maximum 25% individual rate, not flat 25%. Calculate other gain, NIIT and Illinois taxes separately. Mortgage payoff reduces cash, not ordinarily gain.

Before a study or IRA conversion, ask a CPA to model usable deductions, suspended losses, future sale taxes, hold period and costs. See passive losses, cost segregation and the recapture and sale example.

What should Illinois industrial owners account for in retirement?

Retirement does not exempt ordinary rent or property gains from Illinois income tax. Qualifying retirement income included in federal AGI, including eligible IRA/plan distributions, can receive a subtraction; direct rent and personally owned building gains are different. See Illinois Publication 120.

Moving states does not automatically remove tax on Illinois sourced rent or sales. Review residence, sourcing, entity returns, credits and distributions with a CPA. Coordinate gains, Roth conversions and withdrawals with federal tax, Medicare income related premiums and Social Security taxation.

Verify every PIN, current/projected taxes, appeals, applicable Cook incentives, insurance, roof/pavement, fire protection and lease expenses. Incentive expiry or tenant departure can change cash despite unchanged base rent. See property taxes, insurance and industrial diligence.

Who will own and manage the investment if you die or cannot act?

Coordinate beneficiaries, wills/trusts, entity agreements, powers of attorney and management authority; they do not all control the same assets. Give family a file of leases, loans, insurance, tax deadlines, vendors, reserves and signing/payment authority.

Inherited buildings and real estate IRAs have different tax and basis rules. Obtain individual legal and CPA review of trusts, joint ownership, estate inclusion and state estate taxes.

Basis and estate tax are separate. Qualifying inherited property generally receives death date fair market value basis, including potential step downs; lifetime gifts generally retain donor gain basis. Inherited entity interests do not automatically reset building basis. Taxable traditional IRA distributions are income in respect of a decedent, without an ordinary property basis reset.

The 2026 federal estate/gift exclusion is $15 million per individual, subject to prior taxable gifts and other rules. Illinois has a $4 million estate exclusion, no spousal portability and no separate inheritance tax. Revocable trusts alone do not eliminate estate tax. Prepare counsel questions on valuations, filings, basis and liquidity with the estate/inheritance guide.

Many inherited accounts require payout within ten years; exceptions and annual distributions depend on account, beneficiary, death and RMD status. Roth beneficiaries also face requirements. Illiquid buildings/restricted funds complicate compliance; review Publication 590-B before assuming indefinite holdings.

Plan for heir disagreements, buyouts, loan consent, administration and sale if nobody wants management. Cover incapacity and death without informal account access.

A retirement planning checklist for CRE owners and investors

Start before a forced sale, retirement or distribution deadline. Update annually and after lease renewal, refinancing, major projects, family changes or new tax law.

  1. Map ownership. List buildings, entities, retirement accounts, debt obligations, guarantees and beneficiaries; distinguish taxable and account owned assets.
  2. Estimate the household gap. Compare spending, taxes and health care with other income and accessible savings. Not all rent is spendable.
  3. Build the cash bridge. Reconcile leases, actual collections, operating expenses, debt and planned capital or leasing cash needs. Include paid management.
  4. Run adverse cases. Test tenant failure, longer vacancy, tax and insurance increases, capital work and worse refinancing or resale. Identify new cash sources.
  5. Check plan eligibility. Verify compensation, contribution limits, employee coverage, transfers, rollover eligibility and provider investment permissions.
  6. Review account transactions. Obtain specialist review of related parties, services, title, guarantees, account expenses, borrowing and any UBIT filings.
  7. Calendar liquidity. Coordinate valuations, RMDs, loan maturities, capital calls, lease expirations and household withdrawals. Check redemption restrictions.
  8. Compare exits. Obtain property pricing and a CPA estimate of proceeds after debt, costs and taxes. Plan any exchange before closing.
  9. Document continuity. Confirm managers, signers, beneficiaries, estate documents and the family's preference to hold or sell.
  10. Use the right advisers. A broker evaluates the property and sale; a CPA, retirement plan administrator, qualified financial adviser and estate or tax counsel cover their specialties.

Bring advisers a rent roll, leases, three years of operating records, condition reports, basis and depreciation schedules, loan documents, account statements, plan documents, beneficiary forms and household cash needs. Share sensitive records through an appropriate private channel, not a public community post.

Retirement and commercial real estate questions

Is commercial real estate a good retirement investment?

Possibly. Evaluate cash after debt, capital and leasing costs, tenant concentration, liquidity, management and your household portfolio. Rent, distributions and resale are not guaranteed.

Can I buy a warehouse or commercial building with an IRA?

Yes, with custodian support and tax compliance. The IRA owns the investment and receives income/pays expenses. Personal use or disqualified person transactions can jeopardize tax status. Obtain independent tax review before signing.

Can my business rent a building owned by my IRA?

Not necessarily. Disqualified person, control or personal benefit rules can prohibit the lease; market rent does not cure it. Obtain specialist legal review before a related business lease or purchase.

Can rental income fund an IRA or Solo 401(k) contribution?

Ordinary rent generally is neither IRA compensation nor self employed plan earned income. Eligible wages/self employment earnings may qualify under plan, income and annual limits. Real estate professional status alone does not make rent compensation.

Is real estate income in a Roth IRA always tax free?

No. Qualified distributions can be tax free, but unrelated business/debt financed income may incur account tax. Prohibited transactions remain restricted; Roth status removes neither property risk nor all tax obligations.

How do I take required minimum distributions when my IRA owns real estate?

Plan valuation/liquidity before deadlines. Use rent, other eligible IRA assets or custodian supported, properly valued in kind property distributions. IRA and 401(k) aggregation rules differ; illiquidity does not waive RMDs.

Can I move proceeds from a 1031 exchange into an IRA?

1031 exchanges swap qualifying business/investment real property, not retirement accounts. IRA contributions retain eligibility/limits; receiving exchange proceeds can create taxable gain.

Is a triple net industrial lease truly passive retirement income?

Not completely. Expense shifting leaves default, expiry, capital, environmental, administrative and resale risks. Review landlord obligations and budget management; work and costs remain.

Does Illinois exempt rental income because I am retired?

No. Ordinary rent and taxable property gains are not qualifying retirement plan income. Illinois subtracts eligible retirement income included in federal AGI. Have a CPA distinguish property income, account distributions and Illinois sourcing.

Should I sell my industrial building before retirement?

Compare managed ownership, taxable sale and qualifying exchange by after tax cash, lease rollover, capital work, loan maturity and family goals. Plan owner occupant business transitions separately. No exit is universally best.

Is a lazy 1031 a special retirement tax exemption?

No. It informally describes less actively managed replacement property. Eligible DSTs/managed properties retain exchange rules, liquidity restrictions, fees and risk. Neither income nor qualification is guaranteed.

Can depreciation offset my retirement withdrawals?

Generally not without a passive loss exception/allowance. Review material participation, real estate professional status, MAGI, basis and at risk limits before offsetting pension, IRA or conversion income.

Should I order a cost segregation study before retiring?

Compare usable deductions, cost, suspended losses, hold period, Illinois differences and recapture with a CPA. Shorter life assets can accelerate deductions without guaranteeing current savings or improving property cash flow.

Will all my building sale gain be taxed as ordinary income?

Not necessarily. Sections 1245/1250 ordinary recapture, unrecaptured Section 1250 gain and other gain follow different rules. Straight line depreciation does not make all gain ordinary income. Request asset specific federal/Illinois calculations.

Can my estate owe Illinois tax even if it owes no federal estate tax?

Yes. Illinois excludes $4 million without portability; the 2026 federal exclusion is $15 million per individual. Gifts, deductions, ownership and Illinois situs matter; these are not simple property equity deductions.

Do gifting and inheriting my building give heirs the same basis?

No. Gifts generally carry donor gain basis; qualifying inherited property generally uses death date fair market value or applicable alternate value. Accounts/entity interests differ. Coordinate gift, estate and later income taxes before transfer.
