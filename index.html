Choosing the best industrial real estate broker in Chicagoland

Owners often start by searching for the best commercial real estate broker in Chicago. The best broker for one building is not the best for every building: it depends on the property type, size, location, and what you want from the sale. These are the questions worth asking any broker, including me.

How do I find the best commercial real estate broker in Chicago?

Look for a broker who regularly closes sales of your property type, in your size range, in your county. Ask for recent comparable closings rather than total career volume, ask who bought those buildings, and confirm the broker’s Illinois license through the Illinois Department of Financial and Professional Regulation.

What makes the best broker for selling an industrial building?

  • Industrial investment sales focus. Selling a building to an investor or owner-user is different work from leasing space.
  • Buyer reach. Access to private investors, 1031 exchange buyers, and owner-users who buy buildings of your size.
  • Pricing you can check. A recommended price supported by comparable sales, rent analysis, and the way buyers will underwrite the building.
  • A written process. A marketing plan, confidentiality steps for tenants and employees, and regular reporting on activity and offers.

What should I ask a commercial real estate broker before signing a listing agreement?

  • Which similar buildings have you sold in the last few years, at what prices, and to which kinds of buyers?
  • How did you arrive at the recommended price, and what would change it?
  • Who will market the property, and how will you protect confidentiality?
  • What are the fee, term, and exclusions in the listing agreement?

Do I need an investment sales broker or a leasing broker?

If you want to sell the building, whether it is leased or vacant, an investment sales broker prices and markets it to buyers. If you want to fill vacant space or find space for your business, a leasing or tenant-representation broker handles that. Some sales of vacant buildings attract both investors and owner-users, so ask how a broker reaches each.
